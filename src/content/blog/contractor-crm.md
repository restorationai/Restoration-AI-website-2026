## What is a contractor CRM?

A contractor CRM (customer relationship management software) is a system that stores every lead, customer, estimate, job, and conversation for a trade business in one place. It tracks each job from first contact to final invoice, so nothing depends on memory, sticky notes, or one person's inbox.

For contractors, "CRM" usually means more than a contact list. Most tools sold to the trades mix three jobs together:

1. **Sales pipeline.** New leads, estimates sent, follow-ups due, jobs won or lost.
2. **Job management.** Scheduling, dispatch, crew assignments, photos, and notes.
3. **Money.** Invoices, payments, and sometimes job costing.

Some products, like Jobber and Housecall Pro, lean toward scheduling and payments for home service crews. Others, like JobNimbus, are built around a sales and production board for roofing and exterior work. Restoration shops often run purpose-built platforms. I compared those in my [restoration software stack guide](/blog/restoration-software).

## What features should a contractor CRM have?

A contractor CRM should have lead capture, a visual job pipeline, scheduling and dispatch, estimates and invoicing, a mobile app for crews, automated customer texts, and open integrations (API or webhooks). If it cannot accept leads from your phone line and website automatically, every other feature runs on stale data.

Here is how I rank them for a field business:

| Feature | Why it matters | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| **Automatic lead capture** | New calls and web forms become records without retyping | "Leads" means a manual entry form |
| **Pipeline board** | You see every job's stage at a glance | No way to mark lost jobs and why |
| **Scheduling and dispatch** | Crews know where to go next | Separate app needed for the calendar |
| **Mobile app** | Techs add photos and notes on site | Office has to re-enter field notes |
| **Estimates and invoices** | Faster quotes, faster payment | Exports to a spreadsheet only |
| **Customer texting** | Confirmations and reminders go out on time | Texts sent from a tech's personal phone |
| **API and webhooks** | Other tools can push data in | Integrations locked to the top tier |

That last row matters more than most owners think. A [2026 ServiceTitan industry report](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/102046-contractor-ai-adoption-surges-in-2026-report-finds) found only 20% of contractors run on a single platform. Everyone else is stitching tools together. If your CRM will not accept data from the outside, the stitching is a person typing.

Check the plan limits too. JobNimbus, for example, [lists API and webhooks on every plan](https://www.jobnimbus.com/pricing) but caps standard integrations at two on its entry tier.

## What is the best CRM for contractors?

The best CRM for contractors depends on your trade and size: Jobber or Housecall Pro for small home service crews, JobNimbus for roofing and exterior work, ServiceTitan for larger HVAC and plumbing shops, and a purpose-built platform like DASH, Albi, or Xcelerate for restoration. No single tool wins every trade.

| CRM | Best fit | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | Solo operators and small crews that want simple scheduling, quotes, and payments | Advanced automation sits in higher tiers |
| **Housecall Pro** | Home service teams up to about 8 users who want booking and payments in one app | Extra users add up on the top plan |
| **JobNimbus** | Roofing, exterior, and rebuild contractors who run a sales and production board | Pricing is quote-only |
| **ServiceTitan** | Larger HVAC, plumbing, and electrical shops with dedicated office staff | Quote-only, per-technician pricing |
| **Restoration platforms (DASH, Albi, Xcelerate)** | Water, fire, and mold companies working insurance claims | Built for job files, not phone intake |

My honest advice: pick the CRM your crew will actually open on a job site. A "worse" tool that everyone uses beats a powerful one that only the office touches.

Then ask every vendor one question. "When a homeowner calls at 9pm, how does that call become a record in your system?" Most will point to a web form or a manual "new lead" button. That answer tells you where your leak is.

## How much does a contractor CRM cost?

A contractor CRM costs about $29 to $400 per month for small-team tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro, while enterprise platforms like ServiceTitan and JobNimbus quote custom pricing. Most vendors charge by user or by technician, so your real cost grows with your crew.

Here is what the vendors publish as of November 2026:

| CRM | Entry plan | Mid plan | Top published plan | Pricing model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | [Core: $29/mo billed annually, $49 month-to-month (1 user)](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) | Connect: $99 to $299/mo annually (1 to 15 users) | Plus: $399 to $529/mo annually (5 to 15 users) | Tiered by users, extra users $29/mo |
| **Housecall Pro** | [Basic: $59/mo annually, $79 monthly (1 user)](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) | Essentials: $149/mo annually (5 users) | Max: $299/mo annually (8 users) | Extra users $35/mo on Max |
| **JobNimbus** | [Essentials (up to 3 users)](https://www.jobnimbus.com/pricing) | Pro (up to 10 users) | Premium (up to 19 users) | Request pricing |
| **ServiceTitan** | [Starter](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) | Essentials | The Works | Quote-based, per technician |

Three costs rarely show up on the pricing page:

- **Onboarding and data migration.** Moving years of customers and jobs takes time, and some vendors charge for it.
- **Add-ons.** Texting, marketing, and advanced reporting are often extra.
- **Labor.** This is the big one. Someone has to type every phone lead into the CRM. If that person is you at 10pm, your CRM costs a lot more than the subscription.

## Why do most leads never make it into your CRM?

Most leads never reach your CRM because they arrive by phone, and phone calls only become records when a person answers, asks the right questions, and types the answers in. When calls ring out, go to voicemail, or get scribbled on paper, the lead disappears before your CRM ever sees it.

![Empty contractor office at night with a desk phone ringing next to a contractor CRM screen](/blog-images/contractor-crm-section.png)
*The CRM is open. Nobody is there to answer the phone that feeds it.*

Look at the full funnel from [Invoca's benchmark](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026):

1. Only 52% of callers reach a person.
2. Of answered calls from digital marketing, 38% are real leads.
3. 45% of those leads convert on the call.
4. And 55% of home services businesses do not ask leads to book the job.

Every one of those drop-offs happens before a CRM record exists. Your pipeline report shows the jobs you tracked. It cannot show the ones you never caught.

Bad data hurts even when the lead does get in. In Validity's [State of CRM Data Management report](https://www.validity.com/resource-center/the-state-of-crm-data-management-2022/), 44% of respondents estimated their company loses more than 10% of annual revenue because of poor-quality CRM data. For a contractor, "poor data" looks like a missing address, no loss type, or a lead tagged "call back" with no phone number.

Here is my math for restoration. Say your line gets 20 calls a week. If you answer at the 52% benchmark, about 10 calls go unanswered. If just two of those were real water losses at a modest $4,000 residential job, that is $8,000 a week. Over a year, that is more than $400,000 in work your CRM never knew existed.

> In my six years as a restoration tech, the pattern was always the same: the job details lived on the back of a fuel receipt in somebody's truck. The office had a CRM. The CRM just never got the address, the source of the water, or the adjuster's name until days later, if it got them at all.

Speed makes it worse. A [Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 companies](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify a lead as those that waited longer. I cover the full research in my guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead). In an emergency trade, a lead that waits for Monday's data entry is usually gone.

## How do you get phone leads into your CRM automatically?

You get phone leads into your CRM automatically by putting an intake system in front of it: an AI receptionist or answering service that answers every call, asks your intake questions, and pushes the record to your CRM through an integration or webhook. The record exists before the caller hangs up, with no one retyping notes.

![Infographic showing five steps from phone call answered to job details captured, bad leads filtered, CRM record created, and tech dispatched](/blog-images/contractor-crm-infographic.png)
*How a phone lead should flow into a contractor CRM without manual entry.*

Here is the workflow I recommend:

1. **Answer every call in seconds, 24/7.** Nights and weekends included. That is when emergency work comes in.
2. **Ask the same intake questions every time.** Name, property address, service needed, urgency, and source of the problem. For water losses, that includes the category under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500), the insurance carrier, and whether the caller can authorize work.
3. **Filter junk before it hits the pipeline.** Out-of-area callers, solicitors, and jobs you do not do should be tagged and logged, not dumped into your CRM as "new leads."
4. **Push the record to your CRM.** Through a native integration or webhook, so the job exists right away.
5. **Dispatch and follow up.** Notify the right tech, then send confirmations from the record.

## How should you choose a contractor CRM in 2026?

Choose a contractor CRM by matching it to your trade and crew size, confirming it accepts leads automatically through an API or webhooks, and testing it with your real field team before you sign. Then fix your front door, because the best CRM in the world cannot track calls nobody answered.

Use this checklist before you sign anything:

- **Trade fit.** Does it handle your job types, like insurance claims, multi-visit jobs, or recurring service?
- **Field adoption.** Will your techs use the mobile app on a job site, in gloves, with bad signal?
- **Open data.** Does every plan include API or webhook access? Which tier unlocks the integrations you need?
- **Real total cost.** Add users, add-ons, onboarding, and the hours someone spends on data entry.
- **Phone intake plan.** Who answers at 2am, and how does that call become a record?
- **Exit path.** Can you export your customers and job history if you switch?

If you are a restoration owner, I would add one more test. Call your own line after hours. Count how many rings it takes. Then check whether that call shows up in your CRM tomorrow morning. That one test will tell you more than any demo.
