## How do plumbers get more leads?

Plumbers get more leads from five main channels: Google Local Services Ads, Google Ads, local SEO and the map pack, referrals and maintenance agreements, and shared-lead marketplaces. But the highest-return move for most shops is not adding a channel. It is answering more of the calls the channels you already run are producing right now.

Here is the channel list, ranked by how much of each lead you actually own:

1. **Referrals and repeat customers.** The cheapest, highest-closing leads in the trade. A customer who trusts you calls you first and rarely shops on price.
2. **Local SEO and the Google map pack.** Slow to build, then nearly free per call. Reviews and response speed are the fuel.
3. **Google Local Services Ads (LSA).** Pay per lead, and each lead is exclusive to you. The best paid starting point for most plumbers in 2026.
4. **Google Ads.** You own the click and the click-to-call, but competitive metros get expensive fast.
5. **Shared marketplaces (Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor).** Fast volume, lowest ownership. The same homeowner is sold to several of your competitors at once.

Every one of those channels ends the same way: a ringing phone. And [78% of customers buy from the company that responds first](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics). The channel gets the phone to ring. The answer decides who gets paid.

## How much do plumbing leads cost?

Plumbing leads cost between $15 and $150+ per lead in 2026, depending on the channel and whether the lead is shared or exclusive. [Google Local Services Ads average about $57 per lead](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost), while standard Google Ads leads run $120 to $170+, and shared marketplace leads look cheap per lead but cost far more per booked job.

| Source | Cost per lead | Shared or exclusive | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angi | [$15 to $85](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost) | Shared with 3 to 8 contractors | You are racing everyone else who bought the same name |
| Thumbtack | [$35 to $60](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost) | Shared | Prices climb exactly when demand spikes |
| HomeAdvisor | [$80+](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/may-2026/home-advisor-plumbing-leads) | Shared | Same lead sold to multiple pros |
| Google LSA | [~$57](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost) | Exclusive | You are charged per lead, including junk, unless you dispute it |
| Google Ads | [$120 to $170+](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost) | Yours alone | Only converts if someone answers the click-to-call |

Cost per lead is the wrong scoreboard, though. The number that pays your techs is cost per booked job, and there the gap is brutal. [Google LSA books about 44% of its leads for roughly $130 per booked job, while a shared marketplace lead closing near 12% can cost $400 to $1,400+ per booked job](https://gowithhero.com/blog/how-much-do-plumbing-leads-cost). Same trade, same city, ten times the cost, purely because of exclusivity and answer rate.

One more data point for context: when we pulled the ad data on this exact search term, advertisers were paying about $83 for a single Google Ads click on "plumbing leads." Companies that sell leads pay that much just to reach you. That should tell you what one captured call is actually worth.

## Are shared plumbing leads worth it?

For emergency plumbing work, usually not. A shared lead is [sold to three to five contractors at once](https://www.batonleads.com/blog/exclusive-vs-shared-plumbing-leads-why-the-difference-is-worth-thousands-per-month), which turns your "inbound lead" into an outbound footrace. The math is stark: [a shared lead at $60 closing at 25% costs about $240 per booked job, while an exclusive lead at $55 closing at 65% costs about $85](https://www.batonleads.com/blog/exclusive-vs-shared-plumbing-leads-why-the-difference-is-worth-thousands-per-month). Same money in, three times the cost out.

![Stressed homeowner standing in a flooded kitchen at night calling a plumber about a burst pipe](/blog-images/plumbing-leads-section.png)
*A burst pipe at midnight does not go to voicemail. It goes to whoever picks up first.*

Think about what a shared lead really is. A homeowner with a flooding basement fills out one form. Five plumbers get pinged. Now the homeowner is getting five sales calls while water spreads across her floor, and she books whoever reached her first. Everyone else paid for a name. You did not buy a customer. You bought a lottery ticket priced like a customer.

The lead-selling model has drawn regulators, too. The FTC ordered HomeAdvisor, an Angi company, to [pay up to $7.2 million over deceptive claims about the quality and source of the leads it sold to contractors](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-order-requires-homeadvisor-pay-72-million-stop-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement), including claims about how often those leads turned into real jobs.

If you do run shared leads, treat them like the race they are: they only pay off if you respond within minutes, every time, including nights and weekends. Which is exactly where most plumbers fall down.

You get exclusive plumbing leads by owning the channel instead of renting it: Google Local Services Ads, a strong Google Business Profile with steady recent reviews, your own website ranking for "plumber near me," and repeat and referral customers who call you by name. Then you make every one of those leads truly exclusive by answering 100% of the calls they produce.

## Why do plumbers lose the leads they already paid for?

Plumbers lose the leads they already paid for because they are under a sink when the phone rings, and a plumbing emergency will not wait. [95% of emergency callers will not leave a voicemail; they dial the next plumber](https://www.bland.ai/blog/answering-service-for-plumbing-companies). So the highest-ticket jobs, the burst pipes and sewer backups, are exactly the ones most likely to slip away, because the caller is panicked and already scrolling to the next result.

The numbers back it up. [Plumbing businesses lose $50,000 to $125,000 per year to unanswered and missed calls](https://vocalyai.com/blog/why-plumbers-lose-15k-monthly-missed-calls-poor-scheduling), and a single missed emergency call can [cost $750 to $1,200 in direct revenue](https://calljolt.com/blog/plumbing/how-much-does-missed-call-cost-plumbing) before you count the referral and repeat business that walks with it. And speed is not linear: responding within five minutes makes you [21 times more likely to qualify the lead than waiting 30](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics).

In my six years on the truck, the jobs we lost were almost never the small ones. They were the after-hours emergencies with the biggest tickets, the calls that came in while every tech was already elbow-deep in someone else's crawlspace. Whoever answered that call owned that job, full stop. A missed-call text does not save you either, because an emergency caller does not settle into a text thread. She keeps dialing, which is why I call [missed call text back a band-aid, not a fix](/blog/missed-call-text-back). The real answer is on the [answering and dispatch side](/blog/answering-service-for-plumbers): pick up live, triage the emergency, and get a tech moving before she hangs up.
