## What is the best answering service for contractors?

The best answering service for contractors is the one that books jobs instead of taking messages: it answers 24/7, asks trade-specific qualifying questions, dispatches your on-call tech while the caller is still on the line, and writes the job into your CRM. Most live services fail that bar, because they are built to relay messages, not to run your front office.

The market splits into two very different products that get sold under the same name:

- **Message relays.** A human agent answers with your company name, takes down the caller's information, and sends you a text or email. Entry plans start cheap, [some as low as $39 per month](https://www.bland.ai/blog/answering-service-for-contractors), and this is what most of the industry sells.
- **Dispatch-capable services.** The agent (or the AI) qualifies the lead, checks availability, books the appointment, and pushes the job into ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or your restoration CRM. This is the product that actually makes you money, and far fewer vendors deliver it.

Every "best answering service" roundup ranks vendors on price, friendliness, and hold time. Almost none of them score the only outcome you care about: did the caller become a booked job on your schedule without you touching the phone. ## How much does a contractor answering service cost?

A contractor answering service costs [$0.75 to $1.50 per minute](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost) on typical live plans, which works out to roughly [$5 to $12 per handled call](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/), with most home service businesses paying between $95 and $450 per month before overages.

Here is the full 2026 pricing picture:

| Billing model | Typical 2026 rate | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Per minute | [$0.75 to $1.50, up to $2.00 at premium vendors](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost) | Rounding up to the next minute on every call |
| Per call | [$3 to $7+ for calls with intake or dispatching](https://www.ever-help.com/blog/answering-service-pricing-comparison) | "Complex handling" upcharges for exactly the calls you need |
| Monthly plans | [$135 to $450 for most small businesses](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost) | Overage rates once you burn the included minutes |
| Hidden fees | [Setup fees of $50 to $500, holiday surcharges of 1.5x to 2x](https://www.ever-help.com/blog/answering-service-pricing-comparison) | Holidays and nights are precisely when emergency calls happen |

Notice the trap built into per-minute billing: the better the agent does their job, the longer the call, and the more you pay. A thorough intake on a water loss or a storm-damaged roof takes five to eight minutes. At $1.25 a minute, that is $6 to $10 per call whether the caller was a $40,000 job or a solicitor.

For comparison, a full-time receptionist runs [about $41,700 a year](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/receptionist-salary-SRCH_KO0,12.htm) plus taxes and benefits, and still only covers 40 hours a week. ## Can an answering service actually qualify and dispatch contractor leads?

Most live answering services cannot truly qualify and dispatch contractor leads: agents work from generic scripts across dozens of client accounts, so they take down a name and number, mark the call urgent or not, and relay the message. Real qualification (scoping the damage, checking authority to hire, paging your on-call tech until someone accepts) is rare and billed as premium handling.

![Field technician reading a new job dispatch notification on his phone beside his service van](/blog-images/answering-service-for-contractors-section.png)
*The test that matters: does your tech's phone buzz with full job details while the caller is still on the line?*

This gap matters because speed decides who wins the job. Responding to a lead within five minutes makes you [100 times more likely to reach them and 21 times more likely to qualify them](https://www.leadangel.com/blog/operations/speed-to-lead-statistics/) than waiting 30 minutes, and [78% of customers hire the first business that responds](https://verse.ai/blog/speed-to-lead-statistics). Yet [only 12% of contractors respond to leads within five minutes](https://pushleads.com/how-contractor-lead-response-time-is-killing-your-conversions-and-the-5-minute-f/), based on Hatch's analysis of over 132,000 contractor lead campaigns.

A message relay does not fix that. It just moves the delay: the service answers in 20 seconds, then your lead sits in a text thread until you climb off the ladder.

In my six years as a restoration tech, the pattern never changed: the message the answering service relayed at 7 a.m. was usually for a homeowner who had already hired someone else at midnight. The call got answered. The job still got lost.

So when you evaluate any service, live or AI, ask three questions. Does it ask my scoping questions, not a generic script? Does it page my on-call tech and keep escalating until a human accepts? Does the job land in my CRM with the address, the damage type, and the urgency filled in? Anything less is voicemail with a salary.

## What happens to a contractor answering service during a storm surge?

During a storm surge, a live answering service hits the same wall you do: a strong hailstorm can [spike call volume 200% to 300% within hours](https://www.fusioncx.com/blog/home-improvement/roofing/answering-service-for-roofers-stop-losing-jobs-before-storm-season-peaks/), agents can only handle one call at a time, hold times stretch, and the callers who hang up dial the next contractor on Google.

This is the dirty secret of per-minute answering services: they are staffed for average volume, and storms are not average. The night a pipe-freeze event or a hail cell hits your metro is the night you get a year of revenue offered to you in six hours. It is also the night your answering service puts callers in a hold queue, and [around 80% of callers who hit voicemail or give up on hold will not leave a message](https://www.numa.com/blog/22-business-phone-statistics). They just call your competitor.

Some national services staff up for CAT events, and they charge accordingly: surge staffing is exactly what those 1.5x to 2x holiday and after-hours surcharges pay for. You are paying premium rates for capacity that still tops out at however many agents are awake.

Software does not have that ceiling. An AI receptionist answers call number one and call number forty at the same second, at 2 p.m. or 2 a.m., at the same per-call cost. Nights and weekends are where this shows up first, which is why I wrote a separate deep-dive on the [after-hours answering service](/blog/after-hours-answering-service) problem: the biggest emergency jobs almost never happen during business hours.

## How do you switch from an answering service to an AI receptionist?

Switching from an answering service to an AI receptionist takes one onboarding call and a call-forwarding change: you keep your existing phone number, configure your services, pricing, service area, and on-call dispatch order, and the AI is answering live within about 48 hours. No new hardware, no contract, and your old service can run in parallel until you trust the new one.

Here is the switch, step by step:

1. **Map your call types.** List your services, your service area zip codes, and the jobs you do not take. This becomes the AI's qualification logic, including politely ending solicitor and out-of-area calls in seconds.
2. **Set the dispatch order.** Who gets paged first for an emergency, who is the backup, and how the escalation cascades until someone accepts. A summary email at 8 a.m. is not dispatch.
3. **Connect the CRM.** Intake data should land in JobNimbus, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or whatever you run, as a job with real fields, while the caller is still on the line.
4. **Forward the line and test it.** Call your own number. Be a messy caller, interrupt, change your story, present a fake emergency, and then check that the job card and the dispatch ping both showed up with timestamps.
5. **Cancel the per-minute bill.** Once the AI has run alongside your old service for a week or two and captured everything, the last step is the most satisfying one.
