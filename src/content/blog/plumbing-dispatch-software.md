## What is plumbing dispatch software?

Plumbing dispatch software is a tool that assigns plumbing jobs to the right plumber, sets arrival windows, plans routes, and tracks trucks in the field. It replaces the whiteboard, the group text, and the office manager's notepad. Most of it lives inside a field service platform like ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber.

The key word is "assigns." Dispatch software works on jobs that already exist. Look at how ServiceTitan's own help docs describe booking. When a customer calls and is ready to book, ["the Call Booking screen opens automatically"](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/book-jobs-schedule-appointments) for the CSR. The CSR then picks the location, job type, and appointment window and clicks Book Job.

So the order is: someone answers, someone books, and then the board does its job. That works great for a water heater swap next Thursday. It falls apart when a slab leak, two backed-up mains, and a burst supply line all call in the same ten minutes and one person is typing.

Plumbing is an on-call trade by nature. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says most plumbers [work full time, including nights and weekends, and are often on call to handle emergencies](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm). Your dispatch setup has to work at those hours too, not just from 8 to 5.

## What should plumbing dispatch software do?

Plumbing dispatch software should give you a drag-and-drop board, a live GPS map, skill matching, route optimization, mobile job alerts for plumbers, and two-way sync with your invoicing. Shops that run emergency service also need 24/7 call intake, emergency triage, and an on-call escalation chain, which most boards do not include.

Here is the checklist I would use. The top half is what most platforms do well. The bottom half is where plumbing shops actually lose jobs.

| Feature | Why it matters for plumbers | Common in plumbing platforms? |
|---|---|---|
| Drag-and-drop dispatch board | See every job and plumber on one screen | Yes |
| Live GPS map | Send the closest truck to an active leak | Yes, often on mid tiers |
| Route optimization | Fit more drain and fixture calls into a day | Yes, often on mid and upper tiers |
| Skill matching | Keep a sewer camera job with the plumber who has the camera | Varies |
| Mobile alerts and job details | Plumbers see the job without calling the office | Yes |
| Invoicing and pricebook sync | No retyping job data | Yes, inside the same platform |
| 24/7 call answering | Jobs get created at 11pm, not the next morning | Rarely |
| Emergency vs. scheduled triage | Burst pipes jump the line, faucet drips wait | Rarely |
| Cascade dispatch until accepted | Someone always takes the emergency | Rarely |

The big platforms compete hard on the top half. Jobber's [route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/34303089729559) can reorder a day's visits to cut backtracking. Housecall Pro says its [scheduling can assign the closest available tech](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/route-optimization-software/) using live GPS from your team's phones.

## How much does plumbing dispatch software cost?

Plumbing dispatch software costs about $29 to $699 a month for small and mid-size shops on Jobber or Housecall Pro, depending on users and plan. ServiceTitan does not publish prices and quotes per technician. The software is usually the smallest line item, though, next to the people answering the phones.

Here are the published 2026 prices from each vendor's own pricing page:

| Platform | Entry plan | Mid plan | Top plan | How it is priced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) | Core: $29/mo (annual) or $49/mo monthly, 1 user | Connect: $99/mo annual, 1 user; $149 for 5 users | Plus: $399/mo annual for 5 users, up to $699 monthly for 15 | Per plan, by user count |
| [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) | Basic: $59/mo (annual) or $79 monthly, 1 user | Essentials: $149/mo annual, 5 users, adds employee GPS tracking | Max: $299/mo annual, 8 users, $35 per extra user | Per plan, by user count |
| [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) | Starter | Essentials | The Works | "Per-technician pricing," quote only |

A few things the pricing pages do not tell you:

- **Dispatch features move up the tiers.** GPS tracking at Housecall Pro starts on Essentials, not Basic. Check which tier holds the features you need before you compare sticker prices.
- **Users add up fast.** A five-truck shop with an office manager is already past most entry plans.
- **None of these prices include answering the phone.** The software books the call. Somebody still has to take it, day and night.

That last point is the real budget question. If you pay a CSR to answer and an on-call plumber to field overflow at night, that payroll dwarfs a $149 software bill. ## How do you dispatch emergency plumbing calls faster?

You dispatch emergency plumbing calls faster by answering every call in seconds, asking the same triage questions on every call, and pushing true emergencies straight to an on-call plumber's phone with one-tap accept. Speed comes from removing the human relay in the middle, not from a nicer board.

Most shops lose time in the same three places:

1. **The call never gets answered.** It rolls to voicemail or the owner's cell, and the homeowner calls the next plumber on Google.
2. **The triage is inconsistent.** One person asks about the main shutoff. Another forgets. The plumber shows up without the right parts.
3. **The handoff is manual.** Someone answers, writes a note, calls the on-call plumber, waits, then calls the next one when the first does not pick up.

Every one of those steps costs minutes, and a leak does not pause. The EPA says [the average household's leaks can waste more than 9,300 gallons of water a year](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week), and that is the slow drip kind. A burst line is a different animal.

> In my years on the truck doing water mitigation, the plumber was often the first call and we were the second. The plumbers who picked up fast kept their customers and sent us the dry-out. The ones who called back in the morning lost the repair, the referral, and the relationship.

That is the part most plumbing owners undercount. A burst pipe is often a water damage claim, and the Insurance Information Institute puts the [average water damage and freezing claim at $15,400](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance) for 2019 to 2023. When you answer first, you control who the homeowner trusts for the whole job, including the restoration partner you refer. If you pay for calls to fill the board, our guide to [plumbing leads](/blog/plumbing-leads) shows which sources are worth it.

Here is what good emergency triage looks like on the call:

- **Is water actively flowing right now?** If yes, walk the caller to the main shutoff and flag it as an emergency.
- **Where is the leak and what is it?** Supply line, water heater, drain backup, or sewage.
- **What is affected?** Ceilings, floors, finished basement, or a unit below.
- **Can we see it?** A photo from the homeowner tells the plumber what parts to load.
- **Is the caller authorized?** A tenant may need the landlord or property manager to approve work.

## Can an AI receptionist feed your dispatch board?

Yes, an AI receptionist can feed your dispatch board by answering every call, collecting the job details, sorting emergency from scheduled work, and pushing the job into your software before the homeowner hangs up. Your plumbers keep using the board they already know. It just fills on its own now.

Here is how we run it on our platform. It started in restoration, and plumbing is one of the trades we serve now.

1. **Answer every line at once.** There is no hold queue, so a freeze night with ten calls at once gets ten answers.
2. **Triage on the call.** The AI asks your questions, using your services and your pricing, and decides emergency or scheduled.
3. **Screen out bad calls.** Out-of-area callers and services you do not offer get tagged with a reason and logged, so they never clog the board.
4. **Ask for a photo.** The homeowner gets a text link to snap the leak. The photo lands on the job record before the truck leaves.
5. **Push the job to your system.** Job details go by webhook to ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber while the caller is still on the line.
6. **Cascade dispatch.** Emergencies go to plumbers in priority order until one taps accept. Scheduled work gets booked near where a plumber is already working.

## How should a plumbing company choose dispatch software?

A plumbing company should choose dispatch software by testing it on its worst night, not its average day. Pick the board your plumbers will actually use for scheduling and routing. Then make sure something answers, triages, and books every call at full freeze-night volume.

Here is the short list I would run before signing anything:

- **Run the 11pm test.** Call your own number at night. Count the minutes until a plumber has accepted the job.
- **Run the surge test.** Have five people call at once. How many reach a person or an AI, and how many hit voicemail?
- **Ask what happens when plumber number one does not answer.** If the answer is "the office calls the next guy," it is not automatic after hours.
- **Confirm the sync.** Does the job land on your existing board, or does someone retype it at 7am?
- **Price the full loop.** Add the software, the answering, and the dispatch payroll. Then divide by booked jobs.

The demand is not slowing down. BLS projects plumber jobs will [grow 7 percent from 2025 to 2035](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/plumbers-pipefitters-and-steamfitters.htm), well above average. More work means more calls, and more calls means more chances to miss one.

You do not have to rip out ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber. Keep the board. Put an AI on the front of it so the burst-pipe call at 11pm becomes a job on a plumber's phone in minutes, not a voicemail you hear at breakfast.
