## What is an HVAC CRM?

An HVAC CRM is customer relationship management software built for heating and cooling contractors. It stores each customer's contact details, service address, equipment, maintenance agreements, estimates, and job history in one record. Most HVAC CRMs live inside a larger field service platform that also handles scheduling, dispatch, and invoicing.

A generic CRM, like the ones sales teams use, tracks people and deals. An HVAC CRM tracks people, houses, and machines. That last part is what makes HVAC different. The same homeowner might have a 14-year-old furnace in the basement, a newer heat pump upstairs, and a maintenance plan that covers both.

When a good HVAC CRM is set up right, your tech pulls up a customer and sees:

1. **Who they are.** Name, phone, service address, and how they found you.
2. **What they own.** Equipment make, model, age, and warranty status.
3. **What they pay for.** Active service agreement, renewal date, and tune-ups due.
4. **What happened before.** Past repairs, estimates they declined, and notes from the last visit.

That history is gold. A declined replacement quote from last spring is your best sales lead this winter. But all of it depends on one thing: the customer got into the system in the first place. ## What features matter most in an HVAC CRM?

The features that matter most in an HVAC CRM are equipment history tracking, service agreement management, a full customer timeline, good-better-best estimates, two-way texting, and open integrations through an API or webhooks. Without automatic lead capture feeding it, every one of those features runs on data someone typed in late at night.

Here is how I rank them for a service-heavy HVAC shop:

| Feature | Why it matters in HVAC | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| **Equipment history** | Know the unit's age and past failures before the truck rolls | Equipment lives in free-text notes |
| **Service agreements** | Recurring revenue, renewals, and tune-up scheduling | Memberships tracked in a spreadsheet |
| **Customer timeline** | Calls, texts, estimates, and jobs in one view | Call history stored in a separate phone app |
| **Tiered estimates** | Homeowners pick good, better, or best on the spot | Quotes built in Word and emailed later |
| **Two-way texting** | Confirmations and "tech on the way" updates | Texts sent from a tech's personal phone |
| **API and webhooks** | Your phone system can create leads automatically | Open API locked to the top tier |

Pay close attention to plan limits on those last two rows. Housecall Pro lists [Recurring Service Plans and Open API access on its Max plan](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/). Service Fusion lists [Open API Integration only on its Pro plan](https://www.servicefusion.com/pricing). FieldEdge offers [basic service agreements on its Select plan and advanced agreements on Premier and Elite](https://fieldedge.com/pricing/). The feature you care about most is often one tier higher than the price you saw first.

## What is the best CRM for HVAC companies?

The best CRM for HVAC companies depends on your size: Jobber or Housecall Pro for small crews that want simple pricing, Service Fusion for growing shops that want unlimited users at a flat rate, FieldEdge for QuickBooks-centered residential service companies, and ServiceTitan for larger operations with a full office staff.

| HVAC CRM | Best fit | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | Owner-operators and small crews that want a clean CRM, quotes, and invoicing | Check equipment tracking and membership depth before you commit |
| **Housecall Pro** | Small and mid-size residential HVAC shops | Service plans and open API sit on the Max plan |
| **Service Fusion** | Growing shops that hate per-seat pricing | Open API only on the Pro plan |
| **FieldEdge** | Residential service companies that live in QuickBooks | Quote-only pricing; customer portal is an add-on below Elite |
| **ServiceTitan** | Larger HVAC companies with CSRs, multiple trades, and big membership programs | Quote-based; built for bigger operations |

My honest advice: pick the CRM your techs will actually update from the attic. A simpler tool that everyone uses beats a powerful one only the office touches.

Then ask every vendor one question: "When a homeowner calls at 11pm with no heat and nobody picks up, how does that call become a record in your system?" If the answer is "your CSR enters it in the morning," you have found your leak.

## How much does an HVAC CRM cost?

An HVAC CRM costs about $29 to $649 a month on published plans. Jobber starts at $29 a month billed annually, Housecall Pro at $59, and Service Fusion at $220 with unlimited users. ServiceTitan and FieldEdge do not publish prices; both quote based on your team size and add-ons.

Here is what vendors list as of November 2026 (regular prices, not intro promos):

| HVAC CRM | Entry plan | Upper plans | Pricing model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jobber** | [Core: $29/mo billed annually, $49 month-to-month, 1 user](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) | Connect, Grow, and Plus: up to $699/mo month-to-month for 15 users | By plan and user count; extra users $29/mo each |
| **Housecall Pro** | [Basic: $59/mo billed annually, $79 month-to-month, 1 user](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) | Essentials $149 to $189/mo (5 users); Max $299 to $329/mo (8 users) | Flat by plan; Max adds users at $35/mo each |
| **Service Fusion** | [Starter: $220/mo billed annually, $259 month-to-month](https://www.servicefusion.com/pricing) | Plus $339 to $399/mo; Pro $552 to $649/mo | Flat, unlimited users |
| **FieldEdge** | [Select](https://fieldedge.com/pricing/) | Premier and Elite | Request pricing only |
| **ServiceTitan** | [Core](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) (call booking, dispatch, memberships) | Demand AI, Field AI, and MAX packages | Quote-based |

Three costs never show up on the pricing page:

- **Onboarding and data migration.** Moving years of customer and equipment records takes time, and some vendors charge for it.
- **Add-ons.** Customer portals, marketing tools, and extra integrations often cost more.
- **Data entry labor.** Someone has to type every phone call into the CRM. During a cold snap, that someone is usually you, at night, from memory.

That last cost is the one that hurts most. It is also the one you can remove.

## Why do HVAC calls never make it into your CRM?

HVAC calls never make it into your CRM because a call only becomes a record after a person answers it, asks the right questions, and types it in. When peak-season calls ring out, go to voicemail, or get scribbled on paper, the customer never exists in your system, and no report will ever show the job you lost.

![Empty HVAC company office at night during a snowstorm with a desk phone light on and paper service tickets stacked by the CRM screen](/blog-images/hvac-crm-section.png)
*The CRM is open. Nobody is at the desk to answer the calls that feed it.*

The calls don't arrive when your office is staffed. ServiceTitan studied inbound residential HVAC calls on its platform and found the [off-hours share of calls jumped from 9.8% in October to 14.1% in June](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/hvac-summer-after-hours-call-spike), with the biggest cluster between 5pm and 9pm. Heat waves and cold snaps push the phones hardest exactly when your CSR has gone home.

Then there is what happens to the calls you do answer. Invoca found that [55% of home services businesses don't even ask callers to book](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026). A call that ends with "we'll call you back" is a sticky note, not a CRM record.

Here is my math. Say a cold snap sends 100 calls to your shop in one week. At Invoca's 52% answer rate, 48 of them never reach a person. Invoca also found 38% of answered calls are real leads, so call it 18 lost jobs. Angi puts the [average furnace repair at $316](https://angi.com/articles/how-much-does-common-furnace-repair-cost.htm), which is about $5,700. If just two of those were dying furnaces, Angi's 2026 data puts a [new furnace install at $4,814 on average](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-install-new-furnace.htm). That is roughly $15,000 gone in one week, before you count the maintenance agreements those customers would have signed.

Speed matters too. A [Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 companies](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify a lead as firms that waited longer. ## How do you get every HVAC call into your CRM automatically?

You get every HVAC call into your CRM automatically by putting an intake system in front of it: an AI receptionist that answers every call in seconds, triages no-heat and no-cool emergencies, captures the address and equipment details, and pushes a complete record into your CRM through an integration or webhook. The record exists before the homeowner hangs up.

![Five step infographic showing an HVAC call answered, no heat or no cool triaged, address and equipment captured, record created in the CRM, and on-call tech notified](/blog-images/hvac-crm-infographic.png)
*How a peak-season HVAC call should flow into your CRM without manual entry.*

Here is the workflow I recommend:

1. **Answer every call in seconds, all at once.** A cold snap is not ten calls an hour. It is ten calls in the same minute. Your intake has to handle that without a hold queue.
2. **Triage the emergency.** No heat with an elderly resident, no cool in a heat wave, a gas smell, or a routine tune-up request. Each one needs a different response.
3. **Capture the details the CRM needs.** Name, service address, system type, symptoms, and whether they are an existing customer or member.
4. **Push the record and notify the on-call tech.** The job lands in your CRM, and the right tech gets an alert right away instead of at 7am.
5. **Flag anything incomplete.** Calls that end without a booking should land in a review queue, not vanish.

## How should you choose an HVAC CRM in 2026?

Choose an HVAC CRM by matching it to your size and service mix, confirming that equipment tracking, service agreements, and an open API are included on the plan you can afford, and testing it with your real techs before you sign. Then fix your phone intake, because no HVAC CRM can track a customer nobody answered.

Use this checklist before you sign:

- **Size fit.** Will the per-user math still work when you add three techs next summer?
- **Equipment and memberships.** Are equipment history and service agreements built in, or bolted on?
- **Tech adoption.** Will your techs update the record from a crawlspace with one hand?
- **Open data.** Are API access and webhooks on your plan, or only on the top tier?
- **Real total cost.** Add users, onboarding, add-ons, and the hours spent on data entry.
- **Peak-season intake.** Who answers when 40 homeowners call in one evening, and how do those calls become records?
- **Exit path.** Can you export your customers, equipment, and job history if you switch?

Here is one test that beats any demo. The next time a cold snap or heat wave hits your market, count how many calls came in that day. Then count how many new records showed up in your CRM. The gap between those two numbers is what your front door is costing you.
