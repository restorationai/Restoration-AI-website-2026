## TL;DR

Handyman leads come from referrals, Google Business Profile, Google Local Services Ads, and shared marketplaces like Angi and Thumbtack. Paid leads run roughly $10 to $85 each, and most marketplace leads are sold to several pros at once. Handyman tickets are small, so a lead you pay for and lose eats your margin fast. The handyman who wins is the one who answers first, quotes on the call, and books the visit before the homeowner calls anyone else.

## Where do handyman businesses get the best leads?

The best handyman leads come from referrals, repeat customers, and a strong Google Business Profile, because those leads are exclusive and already trust you. Paid sources like Google Local Services Ads, Angi, and Thumbtack add volume fast, but you share most of those leads with competitors and pay whether or not you win the job.

Here are the main sources, ranked by how much of each lead you actually own:

1. **Referrals and repeat customers.** Free, exclusive, and the easiest to close. A past customer calls you first and rarely shops on price.
2. **Google Business Profile and the map pack.** Free per lead once it ranks. Reviews and fast answers are the fuel.
3. **Google Local Services Ads (LSA).** Pay per lead, and the homeowner calls you directly. A good paid starting point for most handymen.
4. **Nextdoor, local Facebook groups, and neighborhood word of mouth.** Cheap, but slow and hard to predict.
5. **Shared marketplaces (Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor).** Fast volume, lowest ownership. The same homeowner request goes to several pros at once.

| Lead source | Typical cost per lead | Do you share it? | Who usually wins it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referrals and repeat customers | $0 | No | You, if you answer |
| Google Business Profile | $0 per lead | Rarely | The best-reviewed pro who picks up |
| Google Local Services Ads | [$15 to $30 for handyman work](https://adaptdigitalsolutions.com/articles/google-local-service-ads-for-contractors/) | No, the caller dials you | The pro who answers live |
| Angi | [$15 to $85 plus about $300 a year](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-angis-list-how-angi-works/) | [Yes, typically 3 to 4 pros](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/angi-leads-for-contractors) | The first pro to respond |
| Thumbtack | [$10 to $75 across handyman categories](https://pipelineon.com/blog/handy-handyman/) | [Yes, customers contact multiple pros](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/) | The first pro to respond |

Notice the last column. Every source ends with a phone ringing or a message waiting. The channel creates the chance. Your answer decides whether you get paid.

## Are paid handyman lead services like Angi and Thumbtack worth it?

Angi and Thumbtack can be worth it for a handyman who responds within minutes and tracks cost per booked job, not cost per lead. They are rarely worth it for a handyman who checks messages at lunch, because the homeowner has usually booked someone else by then.

The model works against slow responders. On Thumbtack, [you are charged when you access a lead, even if it never turns into a job](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/). Housecall Pro's own advice is to respond "ideally within a few minutes" because customers contact several pros and early answers win. Angi works the same way: [each homeowner request typically goes to 3 to 4 contractors at the same time](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/angi-leads-for-contractors).

That means you are not buying a customer. You are buying a seat in a race. If you are the fourth pro to call back, you paid full price for a lead that was already gone.

Here is my simple test for any paid lead service. Pull the last 90 days and divide what you spent by the number of jobs you actually booked from it. If that number is more than about a third of your average ticket, the service is not the problem. Your response time probably is.

Marketplaces also make sense in one specific case: you are new, you have few reviews, and you need volume to build them. Use the platform to get your first 20 or 30 jobs, collect reviews, then shift budget toward channels you own.

## How much should a handyman pay per lead?

A handyman should pay no more than about 15% to 25% of the average ticket per booked job, which usually means $15 to $40 per raw lead for small repair work. The real number to watch is cost per booked job, because a cheap lead you lose costs more than a pricier lead you win.

Start with your ticket size. According to Housecall Pro, [most self-employed handymen charge $50 to $80 an hour, and jobs under two hours typically run $178 to $220](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-to-price-handyman-jobs/). Those are small tickets, so every lost lead hurts.

Now run the math the way I walk owners through it. These are illustration numbers, so swap in your own:

| Scenario | Cost per lead | Win rate | Cost per booked job | Share of a $200 ticket |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slow callback on a shared lead | $25 | 1 in 4 | $100 | 50% |
| Fast callback on a shared lead | $25 | 1 in 2 | $50 | 25% |
| Answered live on an LSA call | $25 | 2 in 3 | about $38 | about 19% |

Same lead price. Wildly different business. The only thing that changed was how fast someone answered and how quickly the job got booked.

## How do you get free handyman leads from Google Business Profile and referrals?

You get free handyman leads by building a complete Google Business Profile, earning steady recent reviews, and asking every happy customer for a referral before you leave the job. These leads cost nothing per call, they are not shared, and they close at a higher rate than anything you can buy.

Start with Google. Per Google's own data, [customers are 2.7 times more likely to consider a business reputable, and 70% more likely to visit, when it has a complete Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10515606?hl=en). For a handyman, "complete" means:

1. **Every service listed.** Drywall repair, TV mounting, door repair, fixture swaps, deck repair. Homeowners search for the task, not the word "handyman."
2. **Your real service area.** List the towns and neighborhoods you actually drive to.
3. **Photos of real jobs.** Before and after shots of your own work, not stock images.
4. **Hours that match how you answer.** If your profile says open, someone needs to pick up.

Reviews are the second lever. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that [97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 68% will only use a business with four or more stars, and 74% look for reviews written in the last three months](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/). Recency matters as much as your star count. A steady trickle of fresh reviews beats a pile of old ones.

The simplest system is to ask on the same day the job is done, by text, with a direct link. The longer you wait, the less likely the customer is to follow through.

Referrals work the same way. Ask in person while the homeowner is happy with the finished work. Leave a card, and ask if there is anything else on their list. Small jobs often hide a second small job.

## Why do handyman leads go cold?

Handyman leads go cold because the homeowner reaches voicemail, gets a slow callback, or never gets a price, so they call the next pro. Most homeowners will not wait hours for a small repair. They book whoever answers, gives a price range, and offers a time.

The data on small business phones is rough. A 411 Locals study of 85 businesses found that [only 37.8% of calls were answered by a live person, 37.8% went to voicemail, and 24.3% got no answer at all](https://getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics). A handyman on a ladder with drywall mud on his hands is not going to be in that 37.8% very often.

Speed decays fast, too. The classic Harvard Business Review study on lead response found that [firms that contacted a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times more likely to have a qualifying conversation than firms that waited even one hour longer](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads). For a homeowner with a leaking faucet, an hour is a long time.

## How do you convert more handyman leads into booked jobs?

You convert more handyman leads by answering every call live, quoting a price range on the first call, and booking a specific time before the homeowner hangs up. A homeowner with a quote and an appointment stops shopping. A homeowner with a voicemail keeps dialing.

Here is the order of operations that wins:

1. **Answer in seconds.** Not in an hour, and not after the current job. Live, on the first call.
2. **Scope the job on the call.** What needs fixing, which room, how many items, and any photos.
3. **Give a price range.** Homeowners want a number. "TV mounting usually runs $150 to $400 depending on the wall and the mount" ends the shopping.
4. **Book a real time slot.** Offer two windows that fit your route, and confirm by text.
5. **Follow up after the job.** Ask for the review and the next job on their list.

The hard part is step one. You cannot answer every call while you are working, and a part-time receptionist for a one-truck business rarely pencils out.
