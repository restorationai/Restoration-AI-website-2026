## What is the difference between flat-rate, per-minute, and per-call AI receptionist pricing?

Flat-rate pricing charges a fixed monthly fee for a bundle of usage, per-minute pricing bills every minute the AI spends on the phone, and per-call pricing bills each answered call (or each unique caller) no matter how long it runs. Most AI receptionists blend a flat base fee with one of the usage models.

Here is how each model behaves for a service business:

| Pricing model | How you are billed | Example | Who it fits | Where it hurts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat bundle | Fixed fee buys set minutes | [Rosie: $149 for 1,000 minutes](https://heyrosie.com/pricing) | Steady, predictable volume | Bundle runs out in a surge |
| Per-minute | Base fee plus each minute used | [Dialzara overage: $0.35 to $0.48 per minute](https://dialzara.com/pricing) | Short, simple calls | Long intake calls add up |
| Per-call | Each answered call | [Smith.ai: $8.50 to $11.50 per overage call](https://smith.ai/pricing) | Low call counts | Expensive at volume |
| Per-caller | Each unique person who calls | [Goodcall: 15 cents to 79 cents per extra caller](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing) | Lots of repeat callers | Storm weeks bring new callers |

Call length is the variable most owners forget. In my years on the truck, no homeowner standing in an inch of water ever explained the problem in 60 seconds. They want to know if it is covered, when you can get there, and whether they should shut off the main. A good intake call for a water loss runs three to five minutes. A plan that looks cheap on two-minute appointment confirmations can look very different on five-minute emergency calls.

That is why the right model depends on your call mix, not on the sticker price. Pull your phone log, count your calls, and note your average call length before you look at any plan.

## What hidden fees should you watch for in AI receptionist pricing?

The fees that inflate AI receptionist bills are overage rates, per-action add-ons like booking or Spanish-language calls, per-text charges, feature limits that force an upgrade, and caps on integrations or call history. Ask for the full rate card in writing before you sign.

Here is each one with real numbers:

1. **Overage rates.** This is what you pay in the month that matters. [Dialzara charges $0.35 to $0.48 per extra minute](https://dialzara.com/pricing), and [My AI Front Desk charges $0.25 per extra minute](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/pricing). Read the overage rate before the monthly price.
2. **Per-action add-ons.** On [Smith.ai's rate card](https://smith.ai/pricing), appointment booking adds $1.50 per call, text or Slack notifications add $0.50, a Spanish-language line adds $1.00, and recording with transcription adds $0.25. The useful parts of the call are the add-ons.
3. **Text message fees.** Confirmations, reminders, and photo-upload links all go out by text. [My AI Front Desk bills $0.04 per SMS](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/pricing) beyond its included credits. That is fair pricing, but it belongs in your math.
4. **Feature and seat limits.** [Goodcall's Starter plan](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing) allows one external tool, one team member, and 30 days of call history. Need your CRM and your scheduling app connected? That is an upgrade.
5. **Unpublished overage.** Some providers list included minutes but not what happens after. If a pricing page does not show the overage rate, ask for it in writing.

None of this means a provider is shady. It means the advertised price is the floor, not the bill. I made the same point about human services in our [virtual receptionist pricing breakdown](/blog/virtual-receptionist-pricing), and the AI market follows the same rules with smaller numbers.

## How does AI receptionist pricing compare to a live answering service or in-house receptionist?

An AI receptionist costs a fraction of the alternatives: about $2.30 to $7 per call for a full-featured plan, versus $8 to $14 per call for a live answering service and about $38,010 a year in wages for one in-house receptionist who covers only 40 of the week's 168 hours.

The in-house math surprises most owners. The [Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median receptionist wage at $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm). That person covers one shift. To cover every hour of the week, you need about 4.2 full-time people (168 hours divided by 40). That is roughly $159,600 in wages alone, before payroll taxes, benefits, sick days, and turnover.

| Option | Typical cost | Hours covered | Handles 10 calls at once? |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-house receptionist | [$38,010 a year per person](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm) | 40 of 168 per person | No |
| Live answering service | $8 to $14 per call ([Smith.ai: $10 per included call](https://smith.ai/pricing)) | 24/7 | Depends on staffing |
| Budget AI receptionist | $29 to $149 a month plus usage | 24/7 | Yes |
| Full-featured AI (book, dispatch, CRM) | About $2.30 to $7 per call, depending on volume | 24/7 | Yes |

Even Abby Connect, which sells both, prices it this way. On its own pricing page, [an AI minute counts as half an Abby minute](https://www.abby.com/pricing/), so the same plan stretches twice as far when AI answers.

## How do you calculate the ROI of an AI receptionist in cost per booked job?

Cost per booked job equals your full monthly invoice divided by the number of jobs the service actually booked. Use the real invoice (base fee, minutes, texts, and add-ons), not the plan price, and count booked jobs, not answered calls. This is the only number that compares every option fairly.

Here is a worked example. Say your company gets 100 real job calls a month, averaging four minutes each, so 400 minutes. For booking rates, I use the numbers we see: message-taking services convert around 40% of calls to booked work, and our book-and-dispatch setup converts 70% or more because the job is booked on the call instead of relayed as a message. I give the budget AI the same 40% because it also takes messages.

| Option | Monthly invoice | Jobs booked | Cost per booked job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live answering service at $10 per call | $1,000 | 40 | $25.00 |
| Budget AI message-taker ($99 plan + 200 overage minutes at $0.25) | $149 | 40 | $3.73 |
| Restoration AI Rapid Response ($597 + 400 minutes at $0.20 + 300 texts at $0.02) | $683 | 70 | $9.76 |

Look closely, because the budget AI wins the cost-per-job column. It loses the only column that pays your bills: 30 fewer booked jobs. At [HomeAdvisor's average water damage restoration cost of $3,868](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/disaster-recovery/repair-water-damage/), those 30 jobs are about $116,000 in work. The $534 monthly price gap is covered by one job, seven times over.

> In my six years as a restoration tech, the jobs we lost were almost never lost on price. They were lost in the gap between "we got your message" and "we are on the way." A cheap AI that takes a message just moves that gap to a nicer voice. The plan that books the job, dispatches the tech, and sends the work authorization before the homeowner hangs up is the one that pays for itself.

Speed is the reason the booking gap is so big. A [Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million sales leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited even one more hour. And people do not wait. [Invoca's 2026 benchmark of 70 million calls](https://www.retaildive.com/press-release/20260710-new-invoca-data-finds-the-best-leads-now-start-in-chatgpt/) found that 44% of callers to businesses never reach a person. In restoration, that caller dials the next company on the list.

If you are paying for leads, this math matters even more. Every missed or unbooked call is ad spend you already paid for. I break down what those leads cost in our guide on [how to get water damage leads](/blog/how-to-get-water-damage-leads).
