## What is missed call text back and how does it work?

Missed call text back is an automation that fires an instant text message to any caller you did not answer. The moment a call rolls to voicemail, the system detects the miss and sends a pre-written SMS like "Sorry we missed you, how can we help?" The goal is to reopen the conversation before the lead moves on.

The mechanics are simple, which is why it is everywhere:

1. **A call comes in and goes unanswered.** You are on a job, on another line, or it is after hours.
2. **The system detects the missed call.** It watches your line and catches the rollover to voicemail.
3. **An automatic text goes out.** Usually within 30 to 60 seconds, sometimes faster.
4. **The caller (maybe) replies by text.** Now you have a thread you can work when you get a free minute.

It is easy to set up and cheap to run, and it does beat a silent voicemail box. Text messages get read: SMS pulls a [45% response rate versus 6% for email](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics), and most people check a text within minutes. If your alternative today is a voicemail greeting that nobody listens to, this is a step up. But "better than voicemail" is a low bar. The question is whether it actually recovers the jobs you are losing.

## Does missed call text back actually recover lost leads?

Missed call text back recovers some leads, but far fewer than the sales pitch implies. Real-world reply rates land around [15 to 25%](https://www.getaira.io/blog/missed-call-text-back), which means 75 to 85% of the people who get your text still walk. You reopened the door, but three out of four callers already left the porch.

Run the honest math. Say you miss 20 real calls in a month. The tool texts all 20. At a 20% reply rate, 4 people text you back. Of those 4, maybe half book. That is 2 recovered jobs. Two is not nothing, and if your average ticket is high, two jobs pays for the tool many times over. I am not telling you it is worthless.

I am telling you it is a screen door on a submarine. Look at what is still leaking:

- The 16 callers who never replied. Most did not wait around. [78% of customers buy from the company that responds first](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics), and by the time your text lands, someone else answered live.
- The callers who read the text as automated and ignored it. People can smell a bot autoresponse, especially in a panic.
- Every emergency caller. This is the big one, and it deserves its own section.

Missed call text back is a recovery tool. It only ever runs *after* you have already lost the call. It is designed around the miss, not around the answer. And in restoration, the miss is where the money already went.

## Why don't emergency callers respond to a text back?

Emergency callers do not respond to a text back because an emergency is not a texting situation. When a pipe bursts or a basement floods, the homeowner is not browsing options and waiting for a reply. They are panicking, and panic dials. They call the next number on Google before your automated text even hits their screen.

![Worried homeowner standing in a flooded kitchen calling another restoration company after a missed call](/blog-images/missed-call-text-back-section.png)
*When water is moving, the caller is already dialing the next company, not waiting for your text.*

Think about the actual moment. Water is spreading across the floor. The caller found you, dialed, and got voicemail. What do they do? They hit back and tap the next result. By the time your "Sorry we missed you" text arrives 45 seconds later, they are already on the phone with a competitor who picked up on the second ring. The [<3% of callers who leave a voicemail](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics) tells you everything: people in a hurry do not leave messages, and they do not sit around answering texts either.

> In my six years on the truck, I never once got a job because we texted someone back. The emergency jobs came from one thing: a human (or now, an AI) picking up on the first or second ring. The homeowner with a flooding kitchen does not want a text thread. They want to hear "We can have a crew there in 45 minutes," right now, out loud.

There is also a trust gap. A distressed homeowner who gets an obvious auto-text can read it as "this company is closed or too busy for me." That pushes them toward the competitor who answered, not toward your thread. Speed matters, and it compounds: firms that make contact within five minutes are [100x more likely to actually connect with the lead](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics). A text you send after the miss is already on the wrong side of that window. ## How do you stop missing calls in the first place?

You stop missing calls by putting a live answer on every ring, 24/7, with enough capacity to handle a storm surge, so no call ever rolls to voicemail to begin with. That means either a fully staffed round-the-clock phone team or an AI receptionist that answers instantly, triages the emergency, and dispatches your crew on the call. The point is to make "missed call recovery" irrelevant, because you stop missing calls.

Here is what actually closes the gap:

1. **Answer live, every time, day or night.** The single biggest lever. A caller who hears a real answer on ring one or two almost never dials your competitor.
2. **Handle surge volume.** A human team drops calls when 20 ring at once during a storm. An AI answers all 20 simultaneously, so peak demand (your best sales day of the year) does not become your worst miss day.
3. **Triage and dispatch on the call.** Capture the loss type, IICRC category, address, and insurance details, then push a work authorization by text and notify your on-call tech, all before the caller hangs up. That is how you lock the job in before a competitor can call back.
4. **Keep text-back as a safety net, not the plan.** Fine as a backstop for the rare edge case. Never as your front line.
