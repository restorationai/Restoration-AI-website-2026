## What is a live answering service?

A live answering service is a company that answers your business calls with real human operators, 24 hours a day, using a script you approve. The operator greets callers in your company name, collects their details, and then takes a message, transfers the call, books an appointment, or pages your on-call person.

The word "live" is the selling point. It promises the caller a human voice instead of a voicemail box or a phone tree.

Here is how most live services actually work behind the scenes:

1. **Shared operators:** One operator usually covers many client businesses. Your call pops up on their screen with your script.
2. **Queue-based answering:** Calls enter a queue and go to the next free operator. Call centers commonly aim to answer [80% of calls within 20 seconds](https://callcentrehelper.com/contact-centre-kpis-industry-standards-168507.htm), with an industry average speed of answer around 28 seconds.
3. **Script-driven intake:** The operator asks the questions you wrote down. Anything off-script gets a polite "someone will call you back."
4. **Message delivery:** You get a text, email, or app alert with the caller's name, number, and a short note.

Look closely at number 2. An 80/20 target means up to one in five callers can wait longer than 20 seconds. On a normal day, that is fine. During a freeze or a storm, it is where jobs leak out.

Per-call plans work out to about $7 to $10 per call, with overage around $8.50 to $11.50. Your real cost depends on call length, overage rates, and how the provider rounds minutes.

## What do live answering services do on emergency calls?

On an emergency call, a live answering service follows your escalation script: it takes the caller's details, flags the call as urgent, and tries to reach your on-call tech by phone, text, or page. If that person does not respond, the operator moves down your contact list and keeps trying.

That is the best case. Here is what usually happens in practice:

- **The operator does not triage.** They rarely know clean water from sewage. The [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) sorts water into Category 1, 2, and 3, and each one changes the equipment, PPE, and urgency.
- **The operator cannot commit.** They cannot promise an arrival time or send a work authorization. So the caller hears, "A technician will call you back shortly."
- **The homeowner keeps dialing.** While they wait, they call two more companies from Google.

The clock matters here. The [EPA says](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Every hour the job sits in a message queue is an hour the water keeps moving.

In my years on the truck, the jobs we lost almost never went to a better company. They went to the company that called back first. A message, however polite, is still a callback race.

## What happens to a live answering service during a storm surge?

During a storm surge, a live answering service is limited by how many operators are on shift, so callers wait in a queue, get put on hold, or hit overflow voicemail. Human call centers staff for normal volume. They cannot add trained operators in minutes when a freeze hits your whole metro at once.

This is not a rare event. After Winter Storm Uri in 2021, Texas saw about [500,000 insurance claims](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/tx/south-texas-el-paso/news/2022/01/05/february-winter-storm-insurance-claims-at-500k), and roughly 85% were property claims like burst pipes and roof damage. Every one of those started with a phone call.

Think about what that does to a shared operator pool. The same operators answering your line are also answering every other plumber, roofer, and restorer they serve in that region. When volume spikes for all of them at once, hold times climb. And remember, three out of four callers hang up on hold.

Here is a simple way to size your risk. Say a hold queue or slow callback costs you just two residential water jobs in a storm month. At $3,000 to $7,000 per job, that is $6,000 to $14,000 lost in one month. One commercial loss can be $25,000 to $100,000 or more. The service fee is not the expensive part. The jobs that slip through are.

## Is a live answering service better than an AI receptionist?

A live answering service is better when a call needs human judgment and empathy, like legal intake or a grieving family. An AI receptionist is better for contractors who need every call answered at once, triaged, and dispatched, because it handles unlimited calls at the same time and can act on the call instead of just recording it.

## How do you choose a live answering service for a contracting business?

Choose a live answering service by testing what happens after the operator answers: whether they can qualify the job, reach your tech fast, handle volume spikes, and get the job into your system. A pleasant voice is table stakes. The outcome of the call is what you are really buying.

Use this checklist before you sign:

1. **Ask about operator load.** How many client accounts does each operator cover? What is your average speed of answer at 2am, not just during the day?
2. **Ask about surge plans.** What happens when every customer in a city calls at once? Is there overflow voicemail?
3. **Ask how they triage.** Will they ask about the water source, affected rooms, and insurance? Can they tell a Cat 3 sewage backup from a clean supply line?
4. **Ask how they escalate.** Do they page one person, or keep going down your list until someone accepts?
5. **Ask what you get after the call.** A text with a name and number? Or a recording, transcript, and a job record in your CRM?
6. **Ask about billing rules.** Per minute or per call? How are minutes rounded? Are spam and wrong numbers billable?
7. **Test it yourself at 2am.** Call as a panicked homeowner with water coming through the ceiling. Time how long until a tech is actually assigned.

That last test beats any sales demo. If the result is a message and a promise, look at a dispatch-first setup instead.
