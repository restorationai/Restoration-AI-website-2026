## What is a 24 hour answering service?

A 24 hour answering service is a company that answers your business phone around the clock, so no call ever hits a dead voicemail box. A live operator or an AI picks up nights, weekends, and holidays, captures the caller's details, and either relays a message to you or dispatches your on-call team.

The reason these services exist is simple. [62% of calls to small businesses go unanswered](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics), and [85% of callers who do not reach you never call back](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics). They dial your competitor instead. Voicemail does not save you either, because [fewer than 3% of callers leave a message](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics).

There are two flavors, and the difference matters more than the marketing admits:

- **Live-operator services.** Real people in a call center answer with your business name. Great for a warm human voice, priced per minute or per call.
- **AI answering services.** Software answers instantly, in a natural voice, with no queue and no hold music. Same behavior at 3am as at 3pm.

But the flavor is not the real dividing line. The real question is whether the service just **takes a message** or actually **triages and dispatches**. For an emergency trade, a message at 2am is barely better than voicemail. The job goes to whoever sends a truck first.

## What actually happens to your 2am calls with a live 24 hour service?

With most live 24 hour services, your 2am call reaches an overnight operator who takes a message and promises someone will call back in the morning. They are not staffed or trained to triage an emergency or dispatch your crew at that hour. So the burst-pipe caller gets a sympathetic note in your inbox, and by sunrise they have already hired whoever picked up and sent a truck.

![Worried homeowner calling for help late at night as water pools near a leaking pipe](/blog-images/24-hour-answering-service-section.png)
*At 2am the caller is not waiting for a morning callback. They are dialing the next company on the list.*

This is the part the "24 hour" label hides. Many services run 8 hours of fully trained daytime staff and 16 hours of a bare overnight crew. That night shift is built to log a message, not to run your intake. They will not ask the IICRC category questions, they will not quote from your price book, and they cannot send a tech to the door.

> In my six years on the truck, the 2am calls were the ones that paid. A flooded basement at 2am is a full mitigation job: air movers running for days, dehumidifiers, an insurance claim behind it. Losing one of those to a morning callback was never a missed message. It was a five-figure job handed to the guy who answered live.

Think about the caller's side too. Water is spreading across the floor. They found you, dialed, and got an operator who says "I will pass this along." What do they do? They hang up and tap the next result. [78% of customers buy from the company that responds first](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics), and a morning callback is not first. This is the exact problem I dug into in our guide to the [after-hours answering service](/blog/after-hours-answering-service): the after-hours call is not a message to relay, it is the job itself.

## What does missing one overnight emergency call actually cost a restoration company?

One missed overnight emergency call can cost a restoration company more than a full year of answering service fees. From my years running crews, a residential water mitigation job ran $3,000 to $7,000, and a commercial or Category 3 loss ran $25,000 to well over $100,000. A single 2am call sent to a morning message queue can be any one of those, gone to a competitor.

Let me build the math the way I actually think about it, from the field, not from a generic "contractors lose $X" stat.

- **Residential water jobs.** Call it $5,000 average. Say you take even 6 real overnight emergency calls a month and your live service only relays messages. If half of those callers hire whoever answered live before you call back, that is 3 jobs a month walking out the door. At $5,000 each, that is $15,000 a month, or $180,000 a year, leaking overnight.
- **Commercial and Cat 3 losses.** These are the five- and six-figure jobs. They almost never survive a morning callback, because the caller (a property manager, a plumber referring you, an adjuster) needs an answer now and moves down their list in seconds. Miss one $40,000 commercial dry-out and you have burned more than a decade of any answering service's cost.

That is why "cheapest 24 hour plan" is the wrong lens. The overnight hours are not the quiet hours in this business. They are the hours the emergency work actually comes in, which makes them the most expensive hours to cover badly.

## How do you set up 24 hour call coverage for a service business?

You set up 24 hour coverage by forwarding your business line to a service that answers around the clock, then configuring how it handles calls: your services, your pricing, your on-call schedule, and your dispatch rules. With an AI answering service, that setup is one onboarding call, and you can be live in 48 hours with no contract.

Here is the order I tell owners to work through:

1. **Demand real 24/7, not "24 hour."** Ask directly: at 2am, does the service triage and dispatch, or just take a message? If it is a message, you do not have coverage, you have a notepad.
2. **Forward your line.** Point your main number (or a dedicated after-hours number) to the service. Keep your published business number the same so customers never see a change.
3. **Load your services and pricing.** So the answer at 2am is specific to your business, not a generic script. This is where an AI beats a rented night operator who does not know your work.
4. **Set your on-call cascade.** Define who gets dispatched first, second, and third, so an accepted job reaches an awake tech in minutes, not a group text nobody reads.
5. **Test the 2am path yourself.** Call your own line at midnight. If the experience is "leave a message," fix it before your next storm does it for you.

Most small shops do not need a 12-person overnight call center. They need every call answered live and dispatched, whichever hour it lands.
