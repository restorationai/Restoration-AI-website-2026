## What is a telephone answering service?

A telephone answering service is an outside company that answers your business phone line when you can't, using live operators who greet callers in your company's name, follow a script you approve, and relay the caller's details to you by text, email, or app. Most bill by the minute or by the call.

The model is about a century old. The [first answering service is believed to have started in 1923](https://myreceptionist.com/2019/12/first-answering-service/), taking calls for doctors who had no way to hear about patients while away from the office. Operators sat at [manual switchboards, plugging cables to connect calls](https://www.history.com/articles/rise-fall-telephone-switchboard-operators), then wrote messages and relayed them later.

The technology changed. The core job mostly did not. The U.S. Department of Labor still groups this work under ["Switchboard Operators, Including Answering Service"](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-2011.00), with about 36,600 people employed in 2024, a median wage of $18.57 an hour, and a workforce projected to shrink through 2034.

Today the phrase covers three different products:

1. **Live operator bureaus.** Shared call centers where operators answer for many businesses at once.
2. **Virtual receptionists.** Premium live services that act more like a remote front desk, screening and transferring calls. I covered this tier in our [virtual receptionist guide](/blog/virtual-receptionist).
3. **AI answering services.** Software that answers every call, holds a real conversation, and can book or dispatch during the call.

## What does a telephone answering service actually do with your calls?

A traditional telephone answering service answers the call, collects the caller's name, number, address, and reason for calling, then delivers that message to you. Some services also transfer urgent calls or page an on-call tech. What most do not do is book the job, quote it, or dispatch a crew while the caller is still on the line.

There are two operating models, and the difference is everything for an emergency trade:

- **Message-taking.** The operator records the details and [delivers them by text, email, or app notification for you to follow up later](https://answering365.com/blog/contractor-dispatch-answering-service/). It is a professional voicemail.
- **Dispatch.** The operator follows your on-call protocol and contacts your tech directly. That costs more: roughly [$5 to $12 per dispatch call versus $2 to $5 for a message-only call](https://answering365.com/blog/contractor-dispatch-answering-service/).

In my six years on the truck, the messages that came through our answering service at 2am were almost always stale by the time anyone read them. The homeowner standing in an inch of water did not wait for a callback. They kept dialing down the Google results until someone picked up and said, "We can be there in an hour."

That is the gap. A message is a record that a job existed. It is not the job.

## How much does a telephone answering service cost?

A live telephone answering service costs about $100 to $1,000 or more per month in 2026, with per-minute plans running [$0.75 to $1.50 per minute](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) and per-call plans ranging from [$1 to $11 per call](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html). Most small businesses land between [$135 and $400 a month](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/).

Here is how the three options stack up on published pricing:

| Option | Typical pricing | What you get per call |
|---|---|---|
| Live operator bureau | [$0.75 to $1.50 per minute](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html), or [$2 to $5 per message call](https://answering365.com/blog/contractor-dispatch-answering-service/) | A relayed message |
| Live dispatch service | [$5 to $12 per dispatch call](https://answering365.com/blog/contractor-dispatch-answering-service/) | A page to your on-call tech |
| Virtual receptionist | Entry plans around [$250 for 50 minutes](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/ruby-receptionist-pricing/) | Screening and live transfers |
| AI answering service | [$50 to $300 a month](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) for most plans | Triage, booking, and dispatch |
| In-house receptionist | [$37,000+ a year plus taxes and benefits](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/) | One shift, five days a week |

Watch the fine print. Some providers [round every call up to the nearest full minute](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/), which quietly inflates the bill when you get lots of short calls. I broke down every hidden fee (setup charges, overage rates, holiday surcharges, 28-day billing cycles) in our [answering service pricing guide](/blog/answering-service-pricing).

## Telephone answering service vs AI answering service: which is better?

For an emergency service business, an AI answering service is better than a traditional telephone answering service because it can finish the job on the first call: triage the emergency, book or dispatch, and lock in the customer. A live operator service is better only if you truly need a human voice for sensitive calls and can call back every message within minutes.

The comparison, side by side:

| | Telephone answering service | AI answering service |
|---|---|---|
| Who answers | A shared operator reading your script | Software trained on your services and pricing |
| Outcome of the call | A message sent to you | A booked job or dispatched tech |
| Storm surge capacity | Calls queue behind other clients | Unlimited calls answered at once |
| Industry knowledge | Whatever fits in the script | Built-in triage (for us, IICRC water categories) |
| Pricing model | Metered per minute or per call | Flat monthly plus low per-minute |

Speed is the reason this matters. A [Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found companies that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as those that waited even one more hour, and more than 60 times as likely as those that waited 24 hours. A message-taking service guarantees a delay, because a human still has to read the message and call back.

> Here is the pattern I saw over and over in restoration: the company that wins the job is not the one with the best reviews or the lowest price. It is the one that turns the phone call into a scheduled arrival before the homeowner hangs up. Taking a good message was never the goal. Booking the job was.

## What does each option cost per booked job?

Cost per booked job equals your monthly phone coverage bill divided by the jobs that coverage actually put on your schedule. It is the only fair way to compare a telephone answering service with an AI, because the cheaper invoice often buys fewer booked jobs.

Here is my worked example for a mid-size service company. Assume 100 calls a month at 4 minutes each, or 400 minutes. These are round numbers for illustration, so plug in your own phone log.

1. **Live operator bureau at $1.25 per minute** (the middle of the [$0.75 to $1.50 range](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html)): about $500 a month, before rounding and overages. At a 40% booking rate after callbacks (the benchmark we use for message-taking coverage), that is 40 jobs, or **$12.50 per booked job**.
2. **Our Rapid Response plan at $597 plus $0.20 per minute:** about $677 a month. That plan includes dispatch, work authorizations, and visual intake. Across our contractor deployments, calls convert at better than 70%, so that is 70 jobs, or **about $9.67 per booked job**.

The AI invoice is higher in this example. The cost per booked job is lower, and there are 30 more jobs on the schedule. In restoration, where an average residential water job runs $3,000 to $7,000 in my experience, even two or three of those extra jobs pay for years of coverage. At higher call volumes the gap widens: our all-in cost lands around $2.32 per call at mid-volume, compared with the $8 to $14 per call that live services typically run.

A note on the numbers you will see elsewhere. Nearly every answering service vendor quotes "85% of callers never call back." An [independent fact-check found no study behind that figure](https://www.majleads.com/blog/ai-receptionist-statistics-fact-check). The number I trust is from a [411 Locals study that monitored 85 businesses for 30 days](https://411locals.us/small-business-owners-dont-answer-62-of-phone-calls/): only 37.8% of calls were answered live, 37.8% went to voicemail, and 24.3% got no response at all. Your own phone log is still the best data you have.

## How do you choose a telephone answering service for a service business?

Choose a telephone answering service by measuring what it does with an emergency call, not what it charges per minute. Pull last month's call log, test each provider with a realistic emergency call, and compare full invoices at your volume against the jobs each option would actually book.

The checklist I give restoration owners:

1. **Count your real calls.** Calls per month, average length, and how many arrive nights and weekends. For round-the-clock trades, our [24 hour answering service guide](/blog/24-hour-answering-service) shows what full coverage should include.
2. **Make a test call at 2am.** Call as a homeowner with a burst pipe. Did they ask where the water came from? Did they get your tech moving, or promise a callback?
3. **Ask what happens in a storm.** When your call volume triples in a day, do your callers wait in a shared queue?
4. **Check what they can finish on the call.** Can they book, quote from your pricing, send a work authorization, and push the job into your CRM? Or only take a message?
5. **Get the full fee schedule in writing.** Rounding increments, overage rates, setup fees, and holiday surcharges.
6. **Compare on cost per booked job.** Use the formula above with your own numbers.

If a telephone answering service passes all six, it is a good fit. If it only takes messages, you are paying someone else to write down the jobs your competitor books.
