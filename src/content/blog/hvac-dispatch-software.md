## What is HVAC dispatch software?

HVAC dispatch software is a tool that assigns heating and cooling service calls to technicians, sets arrival windows, plans routes, and tracks crews in the field. It replaces the whiteboard, the group text, and the dispatcher's notepad. Most of it lives inside a field service platform such as ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber.

The important word is "assigns." Dispatch software works on jobs that already exist. Look at how ServiceTitan's own help docs describe the flow. When a customer calls and is ready to book, ["the Call Booking screen opens automatically"](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/book-jobs-schedule-appointments) for the CSR. Once the job is booked, it ["appears on the Dispatch Board in the office."](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/book-jobs-schedule-appointments)

So the sequence is: someone answers, someone books, and then the board does its job. That works for a maintenance tune-up next Thursday. It breaks down when ten no-cool calls ring at once and your CSR can only type one of them in.

The demand is not going away. The EIA reports that [88% of U.S. households use air conditioning](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=52558). And the Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that HVAC techs [may be on call for emergencies and may work overtime during peak heating and cooling seasons](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm). Peak season is when your dispatch process gets tested.

## What features should HVAC dispatch software have?

HVAC dispatch software should have a drag-and-drop dispatch board, a live GPS map, skill-based tech assignment, route optimization, mobile job alerts, and two-way sync with your CRM and invoicing. HVAC shops that run emergency service should also demand 24/7 call intake and an on-call escalation chain, which most boards do not include.

Here is the checklist I would use, split into what most tools already do and what emergency HVAC work adds:

| Feature | Why it matters for HVAC | Common in HVAC platforms? |
|---|---|---|
| Drag-and-drop dispatch board | See every job and tech on one screen | Yes |
| Live GPS map | Send the closest tech to a no-cool call | Yes |
| Route optimization | Fit more maintenance visits into a day | Yes, often on mid and upper tiers |
| Skill matching | Keep an install crew off a complex commercial repair | Varies |
| Maintenance agreement tracking | Members get priority during a surge | Varies |
| Mobile alerts and job details | Techs see the job without calling the office | Yes |
| CRM and invoicing sync | No retyping job data | Yes, inside the same platform |
| 24/7 call answering and triage | Jobs get created at 2am, not 8am | Rarely |
| Cascade dispatch until accepted | Someone always takes the emergency | Rarely |

The top half is where the big platforms compete, and they are good at it. Jobber's [route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/34303089729559) can reorder a tech's visits to cut backtracking. Housecall Pro says its [scheduling can assign the closest available tech](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/route-optimization-software/) using live GPS from your team's phones.

## How much does HVAC dispatch software cost?

HVAC dispatch software costs about $29 to $700 a month for small and mid-size shops on published plans, depending on users and features. Jobber runs $29 to $699 a month, Housecall Pro $59 to $329 a month, and ServiceTitan does not publish prices; it quotes per technician.

Here are the numbers straight from each vendor's pricing page as of November 2026:

| Platform | Entry plan | Mid and upper plans | How it's billed |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) | Core: $29/mo billed annually, $49/mo month-to-month, 1 user | Connect, Grow, and Plus: $99 to $699/mo depending on plan and team size (up to 15 users) | Flat by plan and user count; extra users $29/mo each |
| [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) | Basic: $59/mo billed annually, $79 month-to-month, 1 user | Essentials $149 to $189/mo (5 users); Max $299 to $329/mo (8 users) | Flat by plan; Max adds users at $35/mo each |
| [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) | Starter (dispatching, scheduling, call booking) | Essentials and The Works | Per technician, quote only; Dispatch Pro listed among its separate "Pro" products |

Two things the sticker price hides. First, the software does not answer your phone. You still pay for whoever books the job, whether that is an in-house CSR, an answering service, or you at midnight. Second, the cost that matters is cost per booked job, not cost per seat. If your board is cheap but a third of your heat-wave calls ring out, it is the most expensive software you own.

## Why does HVAC dispatch software fall short during a heat wave?

HVAC dispatch software falls short during a heat wave because it schedules calls that were already answered and booked, and the bottleneck in a surge is the intake call itself. When ten homeowners call at once, the board can route all ten. But a person still has to answer and type in each one first.

That is my unique take, and it comes from the restoration side of the house. Storm weeks in restoration look exactly like heat waves in HVAC: every phone rings at once, every customer is upset, and every one of them is calling three companies.

> In my years on the truck, our busiest storm weeks were never as profitable as they should have been. We had the crews. We had the equipment. What we did not have was enough hands to answer the phone, so the jobs that rang out went to whoever picked up first. Better scheduling would not have fixed that. Answering would have.

The stakes in HVAC are higher than lost revenue. The CDC says people 65 and older [do not adjust as well as young people to sudden changes in temperature](https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/risk-factors/heat-and-older-adults-aged-65.html), and it tells people not to rely on a fan as their main cooling source in extreme heat. That is why the Kansas City crews were [putting elderly residents and families with health conditions first](https://www.kctv5.com/2026/09/02/heat-wave-pushes-metro-ac-units-hvac-crews-limit/). You cannot prioritize the vulnerable caller if their call went to voicemail.

The fix is to make the board fill the moment the phone rings. That means intake, triage, and job creation happen on the call, automatically, so your dispatcher (or your software) is working on real jobs instead of a voicemail backlog. Speed matters here too. A [Harvard Business Review study of online leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found companies that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as those that waited longer. ## Can AI dispatch HVAC technicians automatically?

Yes, AI can dispatch HVAC technicians automatically if it is set up to do more than book appointments. The AI answers the call, triages no-heat or no-cool urgency, creates the job, picks a qualified on-call tech, and keeps going down the list until someone taps accept. Many AI tools only book; fewer actually dispatch.

That difference matters when you compare options. ServiceTitan now sells an AI voice agent that [books new jobs, including after hours](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/ai-voice-agent-for-basic-phones-and-phones-pro-faq). Per its help docs, assigning the technician is handled separately by Dispatch Pro, and the agent's follow-up call to that tech is ["informational only and cannot be used to reschedule or cancel the job."](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/ai-voice-agent-for-basic-phones-and-phones-pro-faq) The docs also say all calls, regardless of outcome, are included in your usage bill.

Here is how we run it on our platform, which started in restoration and now runs HVAC emergency service too:

1. **Answer in seconds, every line at once.** There is no queue. Ten callers on the hottest day of the year all get answered on the first ring.
2. **Triage on the call.** The AI collects the address, the problem, the urgency, and anything you tell it to ask, using your own services and pricing.
3. **Screen out bad calls.** Out-of-area callers and services you do not offer get tagged and logged, so they never clog the board.

5. **Cascade dispatch.** The job goes to techs in priority order, matched by role and skill, until one taps accept.
6. **Book scheduled work near your techs.** Smart Route Booking puts non-emergency jobs close to where a tech already is.

## How do you dispatch HVAC calls after hours?

You dispatch HVAC calls after hours by routing your phone line to a 24/7 intake system that triages each call and pushes emergencies straight to an on-call tech's phone. Non-urgent calls get booked into the next open slot. The on-call tech should only wake up for real emergencies, not for maintenance questions.

Most HVAC shops handle nights one of three ways. Here is how they compare:

| After-hours setup | What happens to a 2am no-heat call | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|
| Owner or on-call tech's cell | Rings one person; if they miss it, voicemail | One person is the whole system |
| Live answering service | Message taken, on-call tech paged | Callback delays; no booking into your board |
| AI intake plus cascade dispatch | Answered, triaged, job created, tech accepts | Needs your rules set up once |

The rules you set are what keep your techs from burning out. Here is what I would define before going live:

- **What counts as an emergency.** No heat in freezing weather, no cooling for an elderly or medically fragile caller, and anything involving gas or burning smells.
- **What waits until morning.** Maintenance scheduling, quotes, filter questions, and non-urgent repairs.
- **Your on-call order.** Who gets the job first, who is second, and how long each has to accept.
- **Your service area.** Zip codes you cover, so a call 60 miles out does not wake anyone up.
- **What gets logged.** Every call should leave a recording, a summary, and a transcript, so you can review the night in five minutes.

## How should you choose HVAC dispatch software?

You should choose HVAC dispatch software by testing it on your worst day, not your average one. Pick the board your team will actually use for scheduling and routing, then make sure something answers and books every call, every hour, at full surge volume.

Here is the short list I would run:

- **Run the 2am test.** Call your own number at night. Count the minutes until a tech has accepted the job.
- **Run the surge test.** Have five people call at once. How many reach a person or an AI, and how many hit voicemail?
- **Ask what happens when tech number one does not respond.** If the answer is "your dispatcher calls the next person," it is not automatic at night.
- **Confirm the sync.** Does the job land in your existing board, or does someone retype it in the morning?
- **Price the full loop.** Add up the software, the answering, and the dispatcher payroll. Then divide by booked jobs.

You do not have to rip out ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber. Keep the board your techs already know.
