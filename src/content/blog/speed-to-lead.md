## What is speed to lead?

Speed to lead is the time it takes a business to make real contact with a new lead after that lead reaches out. For a web form, it is the gap between submit and first reply. For a phone call, it is the gap between the first ring and a person (or AI) on the line who can actually help.

The idea comes from sales research. A [Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million sales leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited even one more hour. Later data pushed the window tighter: you are [21 times more likely to qualify a lead with a fast response than if you wait more than 30 minutes](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics).

Most businesses still miss it badly. The same Chili Piper analysis found B2B sales teams take an average of 42 hours to respond to a new lead. Contractors are faster, but not fast enough: the average contractor takes [42 to 47 minutes to respond](https://pushleads.com/how-contractor-lead-response-time-is-killing-your-conversions-and-the-5-minute-f/).

Here is the catch. Every one of those numbers measures a lead that waits for you. A restoration lead does not wait.

## Why does speed to lead matter more in restoration than other industries?

Speed to lead matters more in restoration because the lead is an active emergency, not a research project. A homeowner with water spreading across the floor calls the first company they find, and if nobody answers, they call the second. There is no "follow up tomorrow." The job goes to whoever answers first.

Three things make restoration different from almost any other industry:

1. **The damage gets worse by the hour.** The EPA says it is important to [dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). Homeowners know the clock is running, so they will not sit by the phone.
2. **The tickets are big.** The average homeowners insurance claim for water damage and freezing was [$15,400 from 2019 to 2023](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance). One missed call is not a missed $200 service visit.
3. **The partners are watching.** Insurance and referral programs grade you on speed. Rainbow Restoration's key accounts program asks pros to [contact customers within 30 minutes and arrive on scene within two to four hours](https://rainbowrestores.com/national-accounts). Adjusters remember who was slow.

In my years on the truck, I watched this play out over and over. We would roll up to a job and the homeowner would say, "You were the only ones who picked up." That was not a compliment to our marketing. It was a warning about everyone else's phones.

> In my six years as a restoration tech, the pattern was always the same: the company that answered first got the job, and the company that called back got a polite "we already have someone here." Speed to lead in restoration is not measured in minutes. It is measured in rings.

## What is a good speed-to-lead benchmark for contractors?

A good speed-to-lead benchmark for a restoration contractor is answering live on the first or second ring, 24 hours a day, with every caller qualified and dispatched on that same call. The common five-minute rule is a fine target for web forms, but for inbound emergency calls, five minutes means the caller is already talking to someone else.

Here is how I break it down by channel. These are the targets I hold restoration companies to, not an industry standard, and the reasons come from the sources above:

| Lead channel | Common advice | Restoration target | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inbound phone call | "Call back fast" | Answer on ring 1 or 2, every hour of the day | Callers hang up and dial the next result |
| Missed call | Text back in minutes | Zero missed calls | Emergency callers rarely wait for a text |
| Web form or chat | Reply within 5 minutes | Call back within 5 minutes | [21x higher qualification at 5 vs 30 minutes](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics) |
| Adjuster or carrier referral | Same day | Contact within 30 minutes | Matches [program expectations](https://rainbowrestores.com/national-accounts) |
| Google Local Services Ads | "Respond quickly" | Answer every call live | Missed calls hurt your ad ranking |

That last row deserves its own note. Google says your responsiveness to customer inquiries is a ranking factor for Local Services Ads, and that ["missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness."](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) So slow answering does not just lose the call you missed. It pushes your ad down for the next caller too. I cover that loop in more detail in [how to get water damage leads](/blog/how-to-get-water-damage-leads).

Hold time counts as well. [60% of callers won't wait on hold for more than one minute](https://www.ringly.io/blog/call-abandonment-rate-statistics-2026). A phone tree or a busy front desk can blow your speed to lead even when the call technically "connects."

## How do you measure speed to lead on phone calls?

You measure speed to lead on phone calls with three numbers: answer rate (the share of calls a live person or AI picks up), time to answer (seconds from first ring), and time to dispatch (minutes from first ring to a tech accepting the job). Most owners only track the first, and many do not track any.

Here is a simple way to get real numbers this week:

1. **Pull your call logs.** Your phone system or call tracking tool shows every inbound call, its duration, and whether it was answered. Filter out calls under 15 seconds (misdials and robocalls).
2. **Calculate answer rate.** Answered calls divided by total real calls. For context, the home services average is 52%, and it ranges from [32% to 74% across sub-industries](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026).
3. **Split it by time of day.** Look at answer rate from 6 PM to 7 AM and on weekends separately. This is usually where the bleeding is.
4. **Track time to dispatch.** For answered calls, note when the tech actually accepted the job. A call answered in two rings that sits in a voicemail box for 40 minutes is still a slow lead.
5. **Check if you asked for the job.** [55% of home services businesses don't ask leads to buy or book](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026). Answering fast and then saying "someone will call you back" throws the speed away.
6. **Call yourself at 2 AM.** Pretend to be a homeowner with water coming through the ceiling. Time every step until a tech is assigned.

## What does slow speed to lead cost a restoration company?

Slow speed to lead costs a restoration company the full value of every job that goes to a faster competitor, which can easily reach six figures a year. Unanswered callers rarely try again: [85% of callers who reach voicemail or get no answer never call back](https://www.patlive.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/).

Here is my own back-of-the-napkin math for a typical residential water shop. Swap in your own numbers.

- **Missed real calls per week:** 4 (nights, weekends, and "I was on a roof")
- **Share that were real jobs:** 50%, so 2 lost jobs a week
- **Average residential mitigation ticket:** about $4,000 (the low end of the $3,000 to $7,000 range I see on typical residential water jobs)
- **Weekly loss:** about $8,000
- **Yearly loss:** about $400,000

That is before you count the commercial loss that comes in once a year and is worth $25,000 to $100,000 on its own. It is also before you count the adjuster who quietly stops sending you referrals because you were slow twice.

The most common fix I see is a band-aid: an auto-text when a call is missed. It helps a little, but as I explained in my breakdown of [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back), an emergency caller does not wait for a text. They dial the next number.
