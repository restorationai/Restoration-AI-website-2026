## What is a roofing CRM?

A roofing CRM is customer relationship management software built for roofing contractors. It tracks each job from first call to inspection, estimate, insurance claim, production, and final payment. Unlike a generic CRM, it handles roof measurements, claim paperwork, material orders, and crew scheduling in the same system.

A general contractor CRM covers leads and invoices. A roofing CRM adds the parts that make roofing different:

1. **Insurance claim tracking.** Adjuster meetings, claim numbers, supplements, and mortgage checks.
2. **Measurements and proposals.** Aerial or drone roof reports that turn into a priced, signable estimate.
3. **Production.** Material orders, crew calendars, permits, and job photos.
4. **Sales pipeline.** Canvassing leads, inspections set, contracts signed, and close rates by rep.

If you run restoration and roofing under one roof, or you are weighing a broader tool, I compared general options in my guide to the [contractor CRM](/blog/contractor-crm).

## What features matter most in a roofing CRM?

The features that matter most in a roofing CRM are automatic lead capture, a visual job pipeline, insurance claim and supplement tracking, built-in measurements and e-signed proposals, a crew-friendly mobile app, and open integrations through an API or webhooks. Without automatic lead capture, every other feature runs on data someone typed late at night.

Here is how I rank them for a storm-driven roofing business:

| Feature | Why it matters in roofing | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| **Automatic lead capture** | Storm calls and web forms become records without retyping | "Leads" means a manual entry form |
| **Pipeline board** | You see every inspection, claim, and build at a glance | No way to track why jobs were lost |
| **Claim and supplement tracking** | Insurance work lives or dies on paperwork | Claim notes live in a separate spreadsheet |
| **Measurements and proposals** | Faster estimates, signed on the spot | Reports must be downloaded and re-uploaded |
| **Mobile app with photos** | Reps document damage on the roof | Photos sit on a rep's personal phone |
| **API and webhooks** | Your phone system can push leads in | Integrations locked to the top tier |

Watch the plan limits on that last row. [JobNimbus lists API and webhooks as unlimited on every plan](https://www.jobnimbus.com/pricing), but caps standard integrations at two on its Essentials tier. [AccuLynx reserves embedded web forms](https://acculynx.com/plan-options/) and multi-location setup for its Elite plan.

Do not overweight AI features yet. ServiceTitan's [2026 Roofing and Exteriors Market Report](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/roofing-exteriors-market-report-2026) surveyed 1,018 roofing firms and found only 4% use AI features built into their CRM. Most of the AI in roofing today sits outside the CRM, at the front door.

## What is the best CRM for roofing companies?

The best CRM for roofing companies depends on your job mix: JobNimbus for residential roofers who want a flexible sales and production board, AccuLynx for insurance-heavy storm restoration shops, Roofr for small teams that want measurements and proposals in one cheap tool, and ServiceTitan for larger multi-trade exterior companies.

| Roofing CRM | Best fit | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **JobNimbus** | Residential and exterior roofers that run a custom board for sales and production | Pricing is quote-only, integration caps on lower tiers |
| **AccuLynx** | Insurance and storm restoration roofers tracking supplements and mortgage checks | Quote-only, key reports and web forms on Elite |
| **Roofr** | Small and mid-size teams that want measurements, proposals, and a simple CRM | Lighter on job costing until the Scale plan |
| **ServiceTitan** | Larger roofing and exteriors companies with office staff and multiple trades | Quote-based, built for bigger operations |
| **Jobber or Housecall Pro** | Repair-focused crews that look more like home service than storm work | Little built-in insurance claim tracking |

My honest advice: pick the CRM your reps will actually open on the roof. A simpler tool everyone uses beats a powerful one only the office touches.

Then ask every vendor this: "When a homeowner calls at 9pm the night after hail, how does that call become a lead in your system?" If the answer is "your office enters it in the morning," you have found your leak.

## How much does a roofing CRM cost?

A roofing CRM costs anywhere from $0 for Roofr's free Starter plan to about $349 a month for Roofr Scale, while JobNimbus, AccuLynx, and ServiceTitan all quote custom pricing. Most quotes scale with users or locations, so your real cost grows with your sales team and crews.

Here is what vendors publish as of November 2026:

| Roofing CRM | Entry plan | Mid plan | Top plan | Pricing model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Roofr** | [Starter: $0, unlimited users](https://roofr.com/pricing) | Essentials: $249/mo monthly, $209/mo billed yearly | Scale: $349/mo monthly, $299/mo billed yearly | Flat, no per-seat fees; SMS add-on extra |
| **JobNimbus** | [Essentials (up to 3 users)](https://www.jobnimbus.com/pricing) | Pro (up to 10 users) | Premium (up to 19), Enterprise (20+) | Request pricing, 14-day free trial |
| **AccuLynx** | [Pro](https://acculynx.com/plan-options/) | (none) | Elite | Custom quote |
| **ServiceTitan** | [Starter](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) | Essentials | The Works | Quote-based, per technician |

Three costs that never show up on the pricing page:

- **Measurement reports.** Aerial roof reports are often billed per report on top of the subscription.
- **Add-ons.** Texting, marketing bundles, and extra integrations can cost more.
- **Data entry labor.** Someone has to type every phone lead into the CRM. During a storm, that someone is usually you, at night, from memory.

That last cost is the one that hurts. It is also the one you can remove.

## Why do storm leads die before they reach your roofing CRM?

Storm leads die before they reach your roofing CRM because they arrive as a flood of phone calls in the first day or two after hail, and a call only becomes a CRM record when a person answers it, asks the right questions, and types it in. When calls ring out or go to voicemail, the lead never exists in your system.

![Empty roofing company office during a storm with a phone off the hook next to paper inspection notes](/blog-images/roofing-crm-section.png)
*The CRM is open. Nobody is at the desk to answer the calls that feed it.*

Start with the baseline. Invoca's [2026 home services benchmark](https://invoca.com/reports/the-invoca-home-services-lead-conversion-benchmarks-report-2026) found that only 52% of inbound calls are answered by a person. That is on a normal day, not the morning after a hail storm. The same report found 55% of home services businesses do not even ask leads to book the job.

Now picture a storm week. Verisk reported that [16 states had more than 20% of their roofs hit by severe hail in 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/06/01/871878.htm), up from 12 the year before. When that happens in your market, every homeowner on the street calls at once. Your reps are on roofs. Your office manager has three lines blinking.

Here is my math. Say a hail storm drives 60 calls to your line in 48 hours. At the 52% benchmark, about 29 of them go unanswered. Invoca found 38% of answered calls are real leads, so call that 11 real roofs. If just three of those become replacements at Verisk's $17,631 average, you lost about $53,000 from one storm. Your CRM's pipeline report will never show it, because those jobs never became records.

> In my years on the restoration truck, storm weeks looked the same every time: we were tarping roofs until dark, and the phone in the cab rang nonstop. The jobs we lost were never the ones we bid badly. They were the calls we could not pick up, and by the time we called back, the homeowner had a signed contract with whoever answered first.

Speed is the other killer. A [Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 companies](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify a lead as those that waited longer. ServiceTitan's report says [67% of roofing contractors now respond to leads within four hours](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/roofing-exteriors-market-report-2026). Four hours is an eternity when storm chasers are knocking on doors the same afternoon. I go deeper on this in my guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead).

## How do you capture storm leads into a roofing CRM automatically?

You capture storm leads into a roofing CRM automatically by putting an intake system in front of it: an AI receptionist that answers every call at once, asks your intake questions, collects roof photos, and pushes a complete lead into your CRM through an integration or webhook. The lead exists before the homeowner hangs up, with no one retyping notes.

![Five step infographic showing a storm call answered, address and damage captured, roof photos uploaded, lead sent to the roofing CRM, and inspection booked](/blog-images/roofing-crm-infographic.png)
*How a storm lead should flow into a roofing CRM without manual entry.*

Here is the workflow I recommend:

1. **Answer every call in seconds, all at once.** A storm is not ten calls an hour. It is ten calls at the same minute. Your intake has to handle that without a hold queue.
2. **Ask the same questions every time.** Name, property address, type of damage (hail, wind, leak, tree), whether water is coming inside, the insurance carrier, and whether a claim is open.
3. **Get photos before the inspection.** Text the homeowner a link to upload pictures of the damage, so you know which jobs need a tarp crew tonight.
4. **Filter junk out of the pipeline.** Out-of-area callers, solicitors, and jobs you do not do should be tagged and logged, not dumped in as "new leads."
5. **Push the lead to your CRM and book the inspection.** The record, photos, and appointment are in the system while your reps are still on a roof.

## How should you choose a roofing CRM in 2026?

Choose a roofing CRM by matching it to your job mix (retail, insurance, or commercial), confirming it accepts leads automatically through an API or webhooks on the plan you can afford, and testing it with your real sales reps before you sign. Then fix your phone intake, because no roofing CRM can track storm calls nobody answered.

Use this checklist before you sign:

- **Job mix fit.** Does it handle insurance claims, supplements, and mortgage checks if you do storm work?
- **Rep adoption.** Will your reps use the mobile app on a roof, in the sun, with one hand?
- **Open data.** Are API and webhooks on your plan? How many integrations does your tier allow?
- **Real total cost.** Add users, measurement reports, texting add-ons, and the hours spent on data entry.
- **Storm intake plan.** Who answers when 40 homeowners call in one afternoon, and how do those calls become leads?
- **Exit path.** Can you export your customers, photos, and job history if you switch?

Here is one test that beats any demo. The next time hail hits your market, count how many calls came in that day. Then count how many new leads showed up in your CRM. The gap between those two numbers is what your front door is costing you.
