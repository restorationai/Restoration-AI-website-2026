## What is field service dispatch software?

Field service dispatch software is a tool that assigns service jobs to technicians, schedules arrival windows, plans routes, and tracks crews in the field. It replaces the whiteboard and the group text. Most of it lives inside a field service management platform such as ServiceTitan, Jobber, or Housecall Pro.

It helps to start with the job it is automating. The U.S. Department of Labor describes dispatchers as people who ["schedule or dispatch workers, work crews, equipment, or service vehicles to appropriate locations, according to customer requests, specifications, or needs, using radios or telephones."](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5032.00) They also prepare daily schedules, relay messages to crews, and create work orders.

Dispatch software takes over most of that work, except the phone part. Look at how the big platforms describe the starting point. On ServiceTitan, [CSRs "enter the customer information directly into their system, including job type, start date, priority, and more"](https://servicetitan.com/blog/field-service-dispatch-app) before the job shows up on the board. The software is built around a person booking the call first.

That works fine for scheduled work like a furnace tune-up next Tuesday. It is a weak spot for emergency work, where the customer is calling three companies at once and the first one with a tech moving usually wins.

## What features should field service dispatch software have?

Good field service dispatch software should have a visual dispatch board, a live GPS map, automatic or assisted assignment by skill and location, route optimization, mobile job alerts for techs, and two-way sync with your CRM. Emergency trades also need 24/7 call intake and an on-call escalation chain, which most boards do not include.

Here is the checklist I would use, split into what most tools already do and what emergency trades should add:

| Feature | Why it matters | Common in FSM platforms? |
|---|---|---|
| Drag-and-drop dispatch board | See every job and tech on one screen | Yes |
| Live GPS map | Know who is closest right now | Yes |
| Route optimization | Less windshield time, more jobs per day | Yes, often on higher tiers |
| Skill and certification matching | The right tech shows up the first time | Varies |
| Mobile job alerts | Techs get details without a phone call | Yes |
| CRM and invoicing sync | No retyping job data | Yes, inside the same platform |
| 24/7 call answering and triage | Jobs get created at 2am, not 8am | Rarely |
| Cascade dispatch until accepted | Someone always takes the job | Rarely |
| Work authorization on the call | The job is locked in before a competitor calls back | Rarely |

The top half of that table is where the big platforms compete. Jobber's [route optimization](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/34303089729559) can automatically reorder a tech's visits to cut backtracking, and it is included on its Connect, Grow, and Plus plans. Housecall Pro says its [scheduling can assign the closest available tech](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/route-optimization-software/) using live GPS from your team's phones.

The bottom half is where restoration owners get burned. Dispatch is only one piece of it, but it is the piece that touches the customer first.

## How does AI dispatch differ from drag-and-drop dispatch boards?

AI dispatch differs from a drag-and-drop board because it makes the assignment decision itself, starting on the inbound call. A drag-and-drop board shows a person the jobs and techs, and that person decides who goes. AI dispatch answers, scopes the job, picks the tech, and keeps going until someone accepts.

Some of the big platforms already use machine learning on the board. ServiceTitan's Dispatch Pro add-on [runs "thousands of scenarios to find the best technician for every job"](https://servicetitan.com/blog/field-service-dispatch-app). That is real optimization. But it works on jobs that are already in the system.

Fieldcode, which builds dispatch tools for large service teams, explains the sequence plainly: the process starts when ["a ticket is created through a customer portal, integration, service desk, email, phone call, or voice AI agent."](https://fieldcode.com/en/field-service-daily/ai-dispatching-in-field-service-explained) In other words, something upstream has to create the job first.

Here is how the two approaches compare on an emergency call:

| Step | Drag-and-drop board | AI dispatch on the call |
|---|---|---|
| Phone rings at 2am | Voicemail, answering service, or the owner's cell | Answered in seconds |
| Job gets scoped | Someone calls back later | Loss type, source, urgency, and insurance collected live |
| Job gets created | A person types it in | Pushed to the CRM during the call |
| Tech gets assigned | A person drags it to a name | Matched by role, certification, and location |
| Tech does not respond | A person starts calling down the list | Cascades to the next tech automatically |
| Customer commits | Hopefully, when the tech arrives | Work authorization sent by text or email |

In my years on the truck, our dispatch board was the owner's cell phone. If he was asleep, in the shower, or on another call, nothing moved. The software we bought later made the board prettier. It did not fix the fact that every emergency still waited on one person to wake up and decide.

> Here is the pattern I saw for six years: the dispatch decision does not start when a job hits the board. It starts the second the phone rings. Every minute between that ring and a tech tapping "accept" is a minute the homeowner spends calling your competitor. Field service dispatch software optimizes the schedule. It does not shrink that minute.

That is also why I do not think of AI dispatch as a replacement for ServiceTitan, Jobber, or Housecall Pro. It sits in front of them. I go deeper on the call-to-dispatch loop in our guide to the [AI dispatcher](/blog/ai-dispatcher).

## Can dispatch software assign jobs automatically by location and certification?

Yes, dispatch software can assign jobs automatically by location and certification, if you set up tech skills and service territories in the system first. Most platforms can suggest or auto-pick the closest available tech. Fewer can match certifications like water, fire, or mold and keep cascading until someone accepts.

Matching the right tech the first time is worth real money. [Aquant's 2025 Field Service Benchmark Report](https://industrialmachinerydigest.com/software/software-general/aquants-2025-field-service-benchmark-report-reveals-ai-enabling-39-faster-machinery-repairs-and-more), which looked at nearly 160 service organizations, found top performers fix the problem on the first visit 86% of the time, versus 53% for the bottom group. A failed first visit added two more visits on average.

In restoration, a "failed first visit" usually means the wrong person showed up. You sent an assistant tech to a Category 3 sewage loss, or a water tech to a job that turned out to be mold. Time makes it worse. The EPA says to [dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), so a wasted trip can change the whole scope of the job.

Here are the assignment rules we run on our platform, so you can see what "automatic" should look like:

1. **Skill-based routing.** Jobs go only to techs certified for that loss type: water, fire, mold, or custom types you define.
2. **Role-based routing.** Send the job to a Mitigation PM, Crew Chief, Assistant Tech, or Estimator, depending on what the call needs.
3. **Territory rules.** Set zip codes and service areas per office or branch, so the right location gets the job.
4. **Smart route booking.** Book scheduled jobs near where a tech already is. If a tech calls in sick, the AI reroutes.
5. **Cascade dispatch.** Push the job to techs in priority order, 24/7, until one taps accept.
6. **Multi-tier dispatch.** Send a PM right away and queue a crew to arrive later.

Whatever tool you pick, ask the vendor one question: "What happens when the first tech does not answer?" If the answer is "your dispatcher calls the next person," the software is not automatic at night.

## How do you connect phone intake to your field service dispatch software?

You connect phone intake to field service dispatch software by putting an AI receptionist in front of your phone line and syncing it to your CRM. The AI answers, collects the job details, and pushes them into your system. It can also dispatch a tech directly, so the job shows up on your board already assigned.

Speed is the reason to wire this together. A [Harvard Business Review study of online leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that companies responding within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify a lead as those that waited even one hour longer. Insurance programs are stricter. Rainbow Restoration's national accounts program asks pros to [contact customers within 30 minutes and arrive on scene within two to four hours](https://rainbowrestores.com/national-accounts).

Here is the setup, step by step:

1. **Forward your lines.** Send your main number (and any ad tracking numbers) to the AI. Each number can have its own rules.
2. **Load your services and limits.** Tell the AI what you do and do not offer, your zip codes, and your pricing, so it qualifies callers the way your best CSR would.
3. **Add your team.** Enter each tech's role, certifications, territory, and on-call order.
4. **Connect your CRM.** We push job data by webhook to ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, JobNimbus, Albiware, Xcelerate, DASH, Encircle, and DocuSketch, before the homeowner hangs up.
5. **Turn on work authorizations.** The customer signs your contract on their phone by the time the tech arrives.
6. **Review the leftovers.** Calls that did not finish get flagged as "Unreviewed Leads," so you work through them daily and nothing slips.

## How should a restoration company choose field service dispatch software?

A restoration company should choose field service dispatch software by testing it on a real 2am water loss, not a scheduled job. The right setup answers the call, triages the loss, assigns a certified tech who accepts, and gets the job into your CRM without anyone in the office touching it.

Here is the short list I would use:

- **Run the 2am test.** Call your own number at night. Count the minutes until a tech has accepted the job.
- **Check certification matching.** Can it keep a water-only tech off a mold job?
- **Ask about the cascade.** What happens when tech number one does not respond?
- **Confirm your CRM sync.** Does the job land in your existing system, or does someone retype it?
- **Price the full loop.** Add up software, answering, and dispatcher payroll. One human dispatcher earns a [median of $50,340 a year](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/43-5032.00), and that covers one shift.
