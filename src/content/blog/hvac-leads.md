## How do HVAC companies get more leads?

HVAC companies get more leads from five main channels: Google Local Services Ads, Google Ads, local SEO and the map pack, referrals and maintenance agreements, and shared-lead marketplaces. But the highest-return move for most shops is not adding a channel. It is raising the answer rate on the calls those channels already produce.

Here is the channel list, ranked by how much of each lead you actually own:

1. **Referrals and maintenance agreements.** The cheapest and most loyal leads in the trade. A service agreement customer calls you first, every time.
2. **Local SEO and the Google map pack.** Slow to build, then nearly free per call. Reviews are the fuel.
3. **Google Local Services Ads.** Pay per lead, and [each lead goes to one contractor only](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac). The best paid starting point for most shops.
4. **Google Ads.** You own the click and the call, but competitive metros get expensive fast.
5. **Shared marketplaces (Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor).** Fast volume, lowest ownership. The same homeowner goes to several of your competitors at once.

Every one of those channels ends the same way: a phone call. And [78% of customers buy from the company that responds first](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics). The channel gets the phone to ring. The answer decides who gets paid.

## How much do HVAC leads cost?

HVAC leads cost between $25 and $200+ per lead in 2026, depending on the channel and market. [Google Ads leads run $40 to $200+, with competitive metros like Miami, Dallas, and Los Angeles pushing $150 to $200+](https://rsgonzales.com/blog/hvac-cost-per-lead-benchmarks-2026/), while shared marketplace leads look cheaper per lead but cost more per booked job.

| Source | Cost per lead | Shared or exclusive | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angi | [$25 to $120, plus a ~$300 annual membership](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac) | Shared with 3 to 8 contractors | [12-month contracts with a 30-35% early-termination penalty](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac) |
| Thumbtack | [$25 to $80, spiking past $100 in surge weeks](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac) | Shared with up to 4 or 5 | Prices climb exactly when you need volume most |
| Google LSA | [$25 to $75 in some markets](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac), [$113 to $180 in others](https://rsgonzales.com/blog/hvac-cost-per-lead-benchmarks-2026/) | Exclusive | You are charged per lead, including junk calls, unless you dispute them |
| Google Ads | [$40 to $200+](https://rsgonzales.com/blog/hvac-cost-per-lead-benchmarks-2026/) | Yours alone | Only converts if someone answers the click-to-call |
| Yelp Ads | [$66 to $87](https://rsgonzales.com/blog/hvac-cost-per-lead-benchmarks-2026/) | Mostly exclusive | Volume is thin outside big metros |

Cost per lead is the wrong scoreboard, though. The number that pays your techs is cost per booked job, and there the gap is brutal: [roughly $542 on Angi, $250 on Thumbtack, and $168 on LSAs](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac), driven by close rates of [12-18% on Angi versus 28-35% on LSAs](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac).

One more data point for context: when we pulled the ad data on this exact search term, advertisers were paying about $62 for a single Google Ads click on "hvac leads." Companies that sell leads pay that much just to reach you. That should tell you what a captured call is worth.

## Are shared HVAC leads from Angi or Thumbtack worth it?

For emergency service work, usually not. Shared HVAC leads go to [3 to 8 contractors at once](https://astraresults.com/blog/post/june-2026/angi-thumbtack-lsas-hvac), close at 12 to 22%, and land at $250 to $542 per booked job, two to three times the cost of an exclusive LSA lead. They can make sense for filling a slow shoulder-season install pipeline, but they are an expensive way to buy a footrace.

Think about what a shared lead actually is. A homeowner with a dead AC fills out one form. Five contractors get pinged. Now your "inbound lead" is an outbound race: whoever dials fastest wins, everyone else paid for a name. The homeowner, meanwhile, gets bombarded by five sales calls and picks whoever reached her first. You are not buying a customer. You are buying a lottery ticket priced like a customer.

The lead-selling model has drawn regulators too. The FTC ordered HomeAdvisor, an Angi company, to [pay up to $7.2 million over deceptive claims about the quality and source of the leads it sold to contractors](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-order-requires-homeadvisor-pay-72-million-stop-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement), including claims that leads turned into jobs at rates the company could not back up.

If you do run shared leads, treat them like the race they are: they only pay off if you respond within minutes, every time, including nights and weekends. Which brings us to the season when that becomes impossible.

## Why do HVAC companies lose leads during a heat wave?

HVAC companies lose leads during a heat wave because demand triples at the exact moment their capacity to answer collapses. [AC repair demand jumps 266% in July](https://www.memphisacandheating.com/ac-repairs-spike-in-summer-demand-jumps-266-in-july), and in June, [14.1% of inbound calls to residential HVAC shops arrive outside business hours, a 45% jump over the October baseline](https://www.servicetitan.com/toolbox/state-of-the-trades/trends/hvac-summer-after-hours-call-spike). The calls stack up precisely when every tech is on a roof and the office line is already ringing.

![Homeowner calling an HVAC company during a heat wave beside a failing thermostat](/blog-images/hvac-leads-section.png)
*The first heat wave floods every HVAC phone line in town at once. Whoever answers wins the week.*

And a heat wave caller does not wait. [80% of callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a message](https://numa.com/blog/22-business-phone-statistics), and responding [within five minutes makes you 21 times more likely to qualify the lead than waiting half an hour](https://www.chilipiper.com/article/speed-to-lead-statistics). A homeowner in a 92-degree living room with two kids does not leave a voicemail. She calls the next result on Google, and one of your competitors books a job your ad budget generated. A missed-call text does not save you either: heat wave callers do not settle into a text thread, they keep dialing, which is why I call [missed call text back a band-aid, not a fix](/blog/missed-call-text-back).

In my six years as a restoration tech, I watched storm weeks do to our phones exactly what the first heat wave does to yours: the board lights up, the office drowns, and the biggest jobs of the year go to whoever picks up. The surge is not the exception to your lead strategy. The surge is the season your lead strategy exists for.
