## What software do restoration companies actually use?

Restoration companies typically use four to five pieces of software: a job management CRM to run the job, a field documentation app for photos and moisture readings, Xactimate for insurance estimates, accounting software like QuickBooks, and a phone system. Few companies run one platform that does everything well.

Here is the stack I see most often:

1. **Job management CRM.** The system of record. Jobs, contacts, schedules, tasks, and job costing live here. The purpose-built leaders are DASH, Albi, PSA, and Xcelerate. A [2026 industry comparison](https://tygartmedia.com/restoration-company-software-comparison-2026/) calls these four "the serious purpose-built platforms," with tools like Encircle, CompanyCam, JobNimbus, and ServiceTitan solving part of the problem.
2. **Field documentation.** Photos, moisture maps, drying logs, and psychrometric readings. [Encircle's Hydro tool](https://getencircle.com/water-damage-restoration) is a common pick, with digital drying logs, moisture mapping, and an S500 compliant mitigation checklist.
3. **Estimating.** Insurance work runs on Xactimate. Owner Verisk says [80% of top property insurance carriers](https://www.verisk.com/en-gb/products/xactimate/) use its property solutions, so your estimates need to speak that language.
4. **Accounting.** Usually QuickBooks or Sage, synced to the CRM.
5. **Phones.** A desk line, a cell phone, call forwarding, and maybe an answering service.

Notice that the phone sits at the bottom of that list. That is how most owners budget for it too. It is also the only step where a job can disappear before it ever touches the rest of the stack.

## What is the best restoration management software for water mitigation companies?

The best restoration management software for a water mitigation company depends on two things: how much of your work comes through insurance programs, and how big your team will be in two years. DASH fits program-heavy shops, Albi fits retail-heavy shops that want flexibility, and Xcelerate fits operators who want process enforced.

The [2026 comparison from Tygart Media](https://tygartmedia.com/restoration-company-software-comparison-2026/) lays out a simple decision path that matches what I hear from owners:

- **More than 30% of revenue from TPA or program work?** Look hard at DASH. It has the deepest insurance ecosystem ties and compliance tools for carrier checklists.
- **Expect 15 or more users within 24 months?** Get a PSA demo. It bundles accounting, jobs, CRM, and analytics in one ERP-style system.
- **Want process discipline over customization?** Xcelerate builds work orders from estimate lines and keeps techs on your SOPs.
- **Want the workflow to be yours?** Albi is the most customizable of the group.

For water mitigation specifically, add one more filter: how good is the drying documentation? Adjusters pay on what you can prove. If your CRM's moisture logging is weak, pair it with a dedicated field tool like Encircle instead of fighting it.

Xcelerate, Albi, and DASH are full restoration job management platforms, while JobNimbus is a general contractor CRM popular with roofers and Encircle is a field documentation tool. A few details matter more than the sticker price:

- **Xactimate sync direction.** DASH connects to [Xactimate and XactAnalysis](https://content.cotality.com/restoration/dash-product-brochure/integrations), and was the first job management platform to [integrate with XactAnalysis](https://www.randrmagonline.com/articles/86191-dash-integration-with-xactanalysis-now-available). Albi's Xactimate integration is [Pro seats only](https://albiware.com/albi-pricing). Ask every vendor which direction data flows.
- **Seat math.** Per-user pricing looks cheap until you add every PM, estimator, and office admin. Albi's minimum is four seats.
- **Onboarding.** Plan for a real implementation fee and a few weeks of cleanup.

And here is the line I want every owner to notice. Albi's Pro plan lists "Calling & Call Recording." That is useful. But recording a call is not the same as answering it at 2am, qualifying it, and dispatching a tech.

## What does restoration software miss at the first phone call?

Restoration software misses the first phone call because it starts working only after someone creates a job. No CRM answers the 2am call, asks the IICRC triage questions, checks your service area, or dispatches a tech. If nobody picks up, the job never enters your software at all.

Think about what the first call actually has to do. Get the caller's name and address. Find the source of the water. Figure out if it is Cat 1, 2, or 3 under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/page/IICRCS500). Check if they are the owner or a tenant. Get the insurance carrier. Send a tech. Every CRM on the list above has a field for that data. None of them collect it.

That gap is expensive because speed decides who gets the job. A [Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 companies](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found firms that responded within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify a lead as those that waited longer. In an emergency trade, an hour is generous.

In my years on the truck, the jobs we lost almost never died in the CRM. They died in the five minutes between a homeowner calling us and that same homeowner calling the next company on Google.

> In my six years as a restoration tech, the pattern was always the same: the software got the attention and the budget, and the phone got whoever was free. The most expensive gap in a restoration tech stack is not a missing feature. It is the first phone call.

Here is the math I walk owners through. Say your average residential water job is $3,000 to $7,000. Say you miss just two real emergency calls a week after hours, and half of those would have booked. That is about 52 lost jobs a year. At $4,500 each, that is roughly $234,000 in revenue that never touched your CRM, your Xactimate, or your QuickBooks. One commercial loss can be $25,000 to $100,000 on its own. Your numbers will differ, so run them with your own job averages.

You connect an AI receptionist to your restoration software by forwarding your phone line to the AI and linking it to your CRM through an integration or webhook. The AI answers, collects the job details, and pushes a new job record into your CRM before the homeowner hangs up. Nobody retypes anything in the morning.

1. **Forward your calls.** Your number stays the same. Calls ring to the AI 24/7, including storm surges, with unlimited concurrent calls.
2. **The AI runs intake.** It collects the caller's name and address, loss type, source of damage, affected areas, urgency, and insurance carrier and claim status. For water, it asks the triage questions that sort Cat 1, 2, and 3.
3. **It filters the junk.** Out-of-area callers, old damage, tenants without authority, and solicitors get tagged and handled gracefully, so your CRM stays clean.

6. **It locks the job in.** The homeowner gets a digital work authorization to sign on their phone, plus a link to upload photos of the damage.

## How should you choose restoration software in 2026?

Choose restoration software by mapping your full job lifecycle first, from the first call to the final payment, and then picking one tool per stage that integrates with the next. Start with the CRM that fits your insurance mix and size. Then make sure something answers and logs every call into it.

Here is the checklist I would use if I were buying today:

1. **Map your stages.** Intake, job management, documentation, estimating, invoicing. Write down which tool owns each one.
2. **Pick the CRM for your book.** Program-heavy: DASH. Process-first: Xcelerate. Flexible retail: Albi. Big and growing: PSA.
3. **Check every handoff.** Ask how data moves from intake to CRM, CRM to Xactimate, and CRM to QuickBooks. One-way syncs create double entry.
4. **Do the seat math.** Count every user for the next 24 months, not just today.
5. **Close the first-call gap.** Decide who answers at 2am, on Saturdays, and during a storm. If the answer is "my cell phone," that is the weakest link in a stack you are paying thousands a year for.
6. **Measure the leak.** Track how many calls you get, how many reach a person, and how many become jobs. If you are buying leads or running ads, this is where your [water damage lead spend](/blog/how-to-get-water-damage-leads) either pays off or goes to voicemail.
