## How much does a virtual receptionist cost?

A virtual receptionist costs $250 to $1,725 per month in 2026, sold as bundles of 50 to 500 live receptionist minutes, which works out to $3.29 to $5.00 per minute at the entry tiers most small businesses buy. AI receptionist plans covering the same minutes run $99 to $690 per month.

Two things jump out of that table. First, the branded virtual receptionist tier (Ruby, Abby) charges two to four times the [$0.75 to $1.50 per minute](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) that generic answering services charge, because you are paying for polish, scheduling, and warm transfers. Second, the cheapest plans carry the most expensive minutes. The $250 plan sells minutes at $5.00. The $1,725 plan sells them at $3.45. Entry pricing is designed to move you up the ladder.

## How does per-minute virtual receptionist pricing actually work?

Per-minute pricing means your plan buys a fixed bundle of receptionist minutes each month, every answered call draws that bundle down, most providers round each call up to the next full minute, and once the bundle runs out, every additional minute bills at a premium overage rate.

The rounding detail matters more than it looks. Industry pricing guides confirm that [calls are billed to the next nearest minute, so a one-minute-ten-second call bills as two minutes](https://gigabpo.com/virtual-receptionist-cost/). Stack 60 calls a month and rounding alone can quietly consume 15 to 25 minutes of your bundle. That is real money at $3.29 to $5.00 a minute.

Now add call length. In my years on the truck, no panicked homeowner ever explained a flooded kitchen in ninety seconds. Real emergency intakes run four to six minutes, and per-minute billing quietly loves that. A 50-minute Ruby Starter bundle is about 12 four-minute intake calls. If your phone rings 12 times in the first nine days of the month, the remaining three weeks bill at overage rates, and overage minutes run [$2.50 per extra minute at AnswerConnect](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) and [$2.99 per extra minute at Abby Connect](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/abby-connect-pricing/).

## What hidden fees show up in virtual receptionist plans?

Five fees to check before you sign anything: setup charges of $50 to $200, overage minutes at $2.50 to $2.99, per-action fees like $1.50 per appointment booked, monthly feature fees for recording and routing, and premium pricing for bilingual or after-hours coverage.

Here is each one with real 2026 numbers:

1. **Setup and script fees.** Many providers charge [a one-time $50 to $200 fee to configure your account and script](https://gigabpo.com/virtual-receptionist-cost/), and [script development alone can run $50 to $150](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html).
2. **Overage rates.** The per-minute price after your bundle is gone: [$2.99 at Abby Connect](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/abby-connect-pricing/), [$2.50 at AnswerConnect, $2.99 at Moneypenny](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html). On per-call plans it is steeper: [Smith.ai charges $8.50 to $11.50 per overage call depending on tier](https://smith.ai/pricing).
3. **Per-action fees.** On Smith.ai's published rate card, [appointment booking adds $1.50 per call, complex call routing $1.50, a dedicated Spanish line $1.00, and call recording $0.25 per call](https://smith.ai/pricing). The receptionist doing the useful part of the job is the add-on.
4. **Monthly feature fees.** Elsewhere in the market, [call recording runs $10 to $30 a month, intelligent routing $25 to $50, and appointment scheduling $20 to $50](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html).
5. **Bilingual and after-hours premiums.** [Multilingual coverage typically adds 10 to 20% to the bill](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html), and some services still treat nights and weekends as a paid tier. For a field business, that is backwards: the after-hours call is the job, which is why we wrote a whole [after-hours answering service guide](/blog/after-hours-answering-service) about it.

Run the worked example. A $329 Abby Essential plan, plus 30 overage minutes in a busy week ($90), plus a scheduling add-on, invoices well north of $450. The pricing page said $329. Nobody lied. They just showed you the floor.

## What does a virtual receptionist cost per booked job?

Divide the full monthly invoice by the number of jobs the service actually booked, not the number of calls it answered: a $329, 100-minute live plan handles about 25 four-minute calls, and if 40% of those callers end up as booked work, you paid about $33 per booked job before a single overage minute or add-on fee.

That is the honest version. The uglier version is more common. Most virtual receptionist plans do not book field-service work at all. They take a message, and the job then depends on how fast you call back from a crawl space. If the service books zero jobs, your cost per booked job is not $33. It is undefined, and the monthly fee is rent on a notepad.

## How do you avoid overpaying for a virtual receptionist?

Price every plan against your real call traffic, not the provider's example customer: pull last month's phone log, multiply your call count by your average call length, add 20% for minute rounding, and only then compare plans, starting with the overage rate instead of the monthly price.

The five-step version I give owners:

1. **Audit your log first.** Count calls in, average call length, and what share arrive after hours. That is your real minute demand.
2. **Add a rounding buffer.** Billed minutes run higher than talked minutes. Assume 20% inflation on short-call traffic.
3. **Read the overage rate before the plan price.** The overage rate is what you pay in the month that matters. A cheap bundle with a $2.99 overage is an expensive plan wearing a costume.
4. **Get the full fee schedule in writing.** Setup, per-action fees, feature fees, after-hours tiers, bilingual premiums. If it is not in writing, it is in your invoice.
5. **Judge quotes in cost per booked job.** Not cost per month, not cost per minute. If a service cannot book or dispatch work, that number never gets good.
