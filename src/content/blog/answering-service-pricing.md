## How much does an answering service cost?

An answering service costs $150 to $400 per month for a typical small business in 2026, with per-minute plans running [$0.75 to $2.00 per minute](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) and per-call plans running [$0.80 to $4.00 per answered call](https://www.hicira.com/resources/implementation-roi/answering-service-rates). High-volume plans reach [$1,000 or more per month](https://www.alliancevirtualoffices.com/virtual-office-blog/answering-service-pricing/).

## How does per-minute answering service pricing actually work?

Per-minute pricing means you buy a monthly bundle of operator minutes, every answered call draws the bundle down, the provider rounds each call up to its billing increment, and once the bundle is empty, every extra minute bills at a higher overage rate. Your plan price is fixed. Your usage is not.

Three mechanics decide whether that model treats you fairly:

1. **The billing increment.** Providers billing in [1-minute increments round every call up, which can add 20 to 40% to your monthly cost](https://vida.io/blog/answering-service-cost) compared to the same rate billed in 15-second increments. A 65-second call bills as two minutes. Sixty short calls a month, and you are paying for a phantom half hour.
2. **What counts as billable time.** Many services bill agent work time, not just talk time: the operator typing up your message after hangup is on your meter. [Ambs Call Center lists this as one of the four most common sneaky charges](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/blog/how-to-spot-sneaky-answering-service-charges) in the industry.
3. **The overage rate.** Overages typically run [30 to 50% above your base per-minute rate](https://bigoutsource.com/answering-service-pricing-comparison-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026/). The month your phone actually rings, your effective rate goes up, not down.

In my six years on the truck, I never once heard a panicked homeowner describe a flooded kitchen in under two minutes. Real emergency intake calls run four to six minutes, which means a 75-minute bundle is about 15 real calls. If your busy week eats the bundle in nine days, the rest of the month bills at the premium rate.

## What hidden fees show up in answering service plans?

Five fees inflate answering service invoices beyond the advertised price: setup charges of $50 to $500, overage rates 30 to 50% above base, 28-day billing cycles that create 13 charges a year, holiday surcharges of 1.5 to 2x, and per-action fees for transfers and messages. Together, hidden costs commonly add [20 to 40% to the advertised rate](https://vida.io/blog/answering-service-cost).

Here is each one with real numbers:

1. **Setup and onboarding fees.** Providers charge [$50 to $500 upfront](https://blog.salescaptain.com/phone-answering-service-pricing-guide/) to build scripts and configure routing. AnswerConnect's is [$49.99 on its entry plan](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/answerconnect-pricing/).
2. **Overage rates.** The rate that applies in the month that matters. [Base rates of $1.50 per minute rising to $2.25 in overage](https://www.hicira.com/resources/implementation-roi/hidden-costs-of-traditional-answering-services) are typical, and PATLive's overages run [$2.00 to $2.60 depending on tier](https://serviceagent.ai/blogs/patlive-pricing/).
3. **28-day billing cycles.** Some providers bill every 28 days instead of monthly, which quietly creates [13 invoices a year instead of 12](https://bigoutsource.com/answering-service-pricing-comparison-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026/). That is a 30-day month of free revenue for them, printed nowhere on the pricing page.
4. **Holiday and after-hours surcharges.** [Holiday coverage can bill at 1.5 to 2x normal rates](https://bigoutsource.com/answering-service-pricing-comparison-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026/), and some services still treat nights and weekends as a paid tier. For emergency trades that logic is backwards: the 2am call is the job. I wrote a full breakdown of what real round-the-clock coverage should look like in our [24 hour answering service guide](/blog/24-hour-answering-service).
5. **Per-action fees.** Patching a call through to your cell, sending the message by SMS, taking an appointment: each can carry its own per-use charge, [including per-minute transfer fees](https://bigoutsource.com/answering-service-pricing-comparison-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026/) that bill while you talk to your own customer.

Run a worked example. A $250 plan, a busy month with 40 overage minutes at $2.35 ($94), a holiday weekend surcharge, and SMS delivery fees lands well past $370. Nobody lied. They just showed you the floor and let your phone traffic build the rest of the invoice.

## Answering service vs AI receptionist pricing: which is cheaper?

An AI receptionist is cheaper than a live answering service at every published 2026 tier: AI plans covering a small business's full call volume run [$20 to $100 a month at the low end](https://heyrosie.com/blog/answering-service-cost) and a few hundred at the full-featured end, while live services charging $0.75 to $2.00 per minute cost [$8 to $14 per call](/blog/restoration-answering-service-vs-ai) at typical service-business volumes.

The structural reason is simple: a live service pays a human for every minute, so it must meter every minute. Software answers every line at once, so it can price flat. That difference shows up hardest in the two places service businesses feel most:

| | Live answering service | AI receptionist |
|---|---|---|
| Typical billing | [$0.75 to $2.00 per minute](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/answering-service-cost.html) | Flat monthly plus low per-minute |
| Busy-month behavior | Overages at [30 to 50% above base](https://bigoutsource.com/answering-service-pricing-comparison-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026/) | Same rate, every month |
| Surge weeks (storm, freeze) | Calls queue behind other clients | Unlimited concurrent answers |
| What you get per call | A relayed message | Triage, quote, booking, dispatch |

> Here is what six years in restoration taught me about this math: the invoice and the revenue move together. The week a storm hits is the week your calls triple, your per-minute plan blows through its bundle, and the live service queues your callers behind every other client they answer for. The busy month is exactly when metered pricing punishes you and exactly when answering every call pays the most.

## What does an answering service cost per booked job?

Cost per booked job equals your full monthly invoice divided by the number of jobs the service actually put on your schedule, and for a message-taking service that number is usually brutal: a $300 invoice that produces 30 relayed messages and 6 booked jobs is $50 per job, and if nobody calls the messages back fast, it approaches infinity.

That callback step is where the money leaks. [85% of callers who reach voicemail or go unanswered never call back](https://www.patlive.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/), and [78% of customers buy from the first business that responds](https://verse.ai/blog/speed-to-lead-statistics). A message relayed at 8am about a 10pm burst pipe is not a lead. It is a record of a job your competitor took.

For a mid-size service company handling 150 calls a month, that lands around $2.32 per call versus the $8 to $14 per call typical of live services. And because the AI books and dispatches during the call instead of relaying a message, calls convert at better than 70% across our contractor deployments. In restoration, one average $5,000 water job that reached a competitor first costs more than a year of either plan.

## How do you pick an answering service without overpaying?

Price every quote against your own phone log, not the provider's example customer: count last month's calls, multiply by your real average call length, add 20% for rounding, then compare full invoices at that volume, and judge the winner on cost per booked job.

The five-step checklist I give owners:

1. **Pull your real numbers first.** Calls per month, average length, and the share that arrive after hours. That is your demand, and it is the only volume that matters.
2. **Add a rounding buffer.** Billed minutes run above talked minutes. On short-call traffic, assume [20 to 40% inflation from increment rounding](https://vida.io/blog/answering-service-cost).
3. **Read the overage rate before the plan price.** The overage rate is your busy-month price. A cheap bundle with a $2.60 overage is an expensive plan in disguise.
4. **Demand the full fee schedule in writing.** Setup, per-action fees, surcharge calendar, billing cycle length. Ask specifically whether they bill every 28 days.
5. **Judge on cost per booked job.** If the service cannot book, quote, or dispatch, the messages it takes still depend on you calling back from a job site, and the math above says most of those callers are already gone.
