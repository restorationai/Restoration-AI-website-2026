## What are Google Local Services Ads?

Google Local Services Ads are pay-per-lead ads that appear at the top of Google Search and Google Maps for local service searches like "water damage restoration near me." You pay only when a customer calls, messages, or books through the ad, not when they click. Businesses that pass Google's screening show a "Google Verified" badge.

That badge is the big difference from regular Google Ads. Google says the [Google Verified badge](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) "signals to consumers that your business has passed Google's proprietary screening process." Older articles call it Google Guaranteed or Google Screened. The current help pages use "Google Verified."

Water damage is one of the [eligible service categories](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en). To start, you need a license or business registration, billing set up, a budget, and reviews where your category requires them.

Here is how LSAs compare to the other paid option most restorers use:

| | Local Services Ads | Google Search Ads |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for | Each valid lead | Each click |
| Placement | Very top of Search and Maps | Below LSAs |
| Trust signal | Google Verified badge | None |
| Keyword control | Job types only | Full keyword control |
| Bad lead protection | Automatic credits only (2026) | Negative keywords |

## How much do Google Local Services Ads cost per lead?

Google Local Services Ads for water damage restoration typically cost about $40 to $180 per lead, depending on your market and competition. Busy metro areas run higher. Google does not publish a fixed price; it says lead prices vary by location, job type, lead type, and bidding mode.

Agency benchmarks give a range rather than one number. PushLeads puts restoration LSA leads at [$40 to $120](https://pushleads.com/google-local-service-ads-for-restoration-companies-the-pay-per-lead-system-that-works-for-emergency-jobs/). Tygart Media reports [roughly $80 to $180](https://tygartmedia.com/restoration-marketing-channels-comparison/), with stable markets closer to $100. Either way, that is far cheaper than search clicks, which run [$91 to $250 or more](https://almcorp.com/blog/water-damage-restoration-cpc-costs-digital-marketing/) for water damage keywords.

What you actually pay for, per [Google's lead rules](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435?hl=en):

1. **You answer a call** or call the customer back and talk to them.
2. **You miss a call during business hours** and the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds.
3. **You get a voicemail, text, or email** from the customer.
4. **You get a booking request** (U.S. and Canada only).

One bit of good news: if the same customer calls back within 15 days, you are not charged again, as long as there was interaction.

Now look at rules 2 and 3 again. A missed call and a voicemail cost the same as a booked job. The average homeowners water damage and freezing claim was about [$15,400 from 2019 to 2023](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance). Losing one of those after paying for the lead is the most expensive outcome in your whole marketing budget.

## What changed with Local Services Ads in 2026?

In 2026, Google began moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as a special Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. You still pay per valid lead, but manual bidding and manual lead disputes are being retired, and the old LSA dashboard goes away after your account migrates.

Here is the timeline from [Google's migration page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585):

- **August 2026:** First wave, for select U.S. home service categories like plumbing, HVAC, roofing, and pest control.
- **Late 2026:** Service-area businesses without physical storefronts. That describes most restoration companies.
- **2027:** All remaining business categories and non-U.S. accounts.

What it means for you:

1. **Download your old reports now.** Google says historical reports won't carry over. Your lead history does.
2. **Expect automated bidding.** Manual bidding, like setting a maximum cost per lead, is no longer supported.
3. **Weekly budgets become daily averages.** Your weekly budget is divided by seven.
4. **Manual disputes are gone.** Google's migration page says it plainly: "Manual lead disputes are no longer supported."
5. **Fix your hours and address in Google Business Profile.** Those sync one way, into the ad.

You get an email 14 days before your migration date. Don't wait for it. Pull your reports this week.

## How do you rank higher in Google Local Services Ads?

You rank higher in Google Local Services Ads by improving your bid, your reviews, and your responsiveness. Google ranks ads through an auction based on your bid, how likely you are to get a lead, and your profile quality, which includes your rating, number of reviews, response time, photos, and verification checks.

That comes straight from [Google's ad ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en). The line that matters most for restorers: "Missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness."

Notice what is missing: distance. Google [removed proximity from its ranking explanation in 2024](https://seroundtable.com/google-local-service-ads-proximity-37366.html). Being closest to the caller is no longer something you can count on.

Google's own [tips to improve LSA performance](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12492201) are worth following:

1. **Get reviews.** Google recommends 5 or more. Treat 5 as the floor, not the goal. My guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) covers how to ask every customer, the right way.
2. **Use "Maximize Leads" bidding.** Google recommends it over manual bids, and manual bids are going away anyway.
3. **Add real photos.** Your trucks, your crew, your equipment. No customer faces or personal details.
4. **Turn on message leads.** Google says they typically cost less than phone leads.
5. **Answer every call.** This one is not on Google's tip list, but it is in the ranking rules. Responsiveness is the factor you control fully, starting tonight.

## Can you still dispute bad leads in Local Services Ads?

Mostly, no. Google now reviews leads automatically, does not charge for leads it judges invalid, and issues credits within about 30 days for leads it later finds low quality. Manual disputes are not supported after the 2026 migration, and Google no longer credits "job type not serviced" or "geo not serviced" leads.

That comes from Google's [lead credit page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/15100654?hl=en). Spam and obvious junk may still get credited automatically. But the caller from two counties over, or the tenant asking about a carpet stain? You may be paying for those now, with no form to fill out.

So the defense has to move upstream:

1. **Set your service area and job types carefully.** Google suggests going broad. That is fine for reach, but every area you add is an area you pay for, credited or not.
2. **Screen bad calls in seconds.** Out-of-area callers, old damage, tenants without authority, and solicitors should be identified on the first call, not after a tech drives out.
3. **Rate your leads.** Google offers a "Rate this lead" survey and says it may occasionally credit leads you report as poor quality.
4. **Log the reason for every bad lead.** If 20% of your LSA calls are out of area, that is a settings problem, and you can only see it with a log.

## Why does answering speed matter more than your bid?

Answering speed matters more than your bid because LSAs charge you for the lead whether or not you win the job. A higher bid buys more calls, but if those calls ring out, you pay full price for leads that hire someone else. Fast answers also protect the responsiveness score that helps your ranking.

The buyer data backs this up. [78% of customers hire the first business that responds](https://verse.ai/blog/speed-to-lead-statistics). And [85% of callers who reach voicemail or get no answer never call back](https://www.patlive.com/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/). A [Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million sales leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found that firms contacting leads within an hour were nearly seven times more likely to qualify them than firms that waited longer.

## Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for restoration companies?

Google Local Services Ads are worth it for most restoration companies because they are usually the cheapest paid source of emergency calls, and the Verified badge builds trust at the top of the page. They only pay off, though, if you answer nearly every call and screen out bad leads fast.

Here is my order of operations for 2026:

1. **Get verified and live** if you aren't already. Screening takes time, so start before storm season.
2. **Back up your LSA reports** before your Performance Max migration.
3. **Set accurate hours, service areas, and job types.** Every setting is now a cost control.
4. **Cover the phone 24/7** so no LSA call goes to voicemail.
5. **Log every bad lead** and adjust settings monthly.
6. **Build reviews steadily**, from every customer.
